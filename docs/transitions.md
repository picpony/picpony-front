# Transitions between screens

> Split out of `AGENTS.md`; read on demand. Cross-references such as "see Motion" or "under Shape" name sections listed in the index in `AGENTS.md`.

> **Timing (checked against the code, 2026-10-23):** every duration here follows `lib/spring.ts`. The hero flight and every container transform ride `FLIGHT_SPRING` (~268ms); the tab strip rides `PAGER_SPRING` (~424ms); a route change is a fade through — the leaving page out on `fastEffects` (~138ms), the arriving one in on `slowEffects` (~300ms) after `HANDOFF_MS` (~69ms). Millisecond figures inside measurement paragraphs are readings taken at the time, not targets.

Five mechanisms, in order of how much they own:

**A forum post opens and closes as a container transform — the hero's construction in miniature — and it moves on its first frame.** One rounded container carries the pressed row into the post card and the card back into its row, riding the route cross-fade (`lib/forumTransitionPlay.ts`; geometry, clock and windows in `lib/forumContainer.ts`, tested in `scripts/testForum.mjs`). The contents are copies on an overlay above the cross-fade's layer — the leaving one from the cross-fade's still frame, the arriving one from the live element, which is hidden for the run — **anchored at the container's top left and never scaled**, so the post's title slides into the row's and cross-fades into it. **The two contents hand over one after the other, on the picture flight's own windows** (`FORUM_FADE`): opening, the row's content goes over the first 15% of the travel and the card's comes in over 15–85% (`HERO_CONTENT_FADE.forward`); returning, the card's goes from the first frame to 60% and the row's comes in over 60–90% (`HERO_CONTENT_FADE.back`, `HERO_CONTAINER_FADE.back`), over the container's own surface, which is there from the first frame to the last. They never cross: two unscaled contents anchored at one corner would double every line that does not line up. The clip is a chain of translated boxes, each owning one or two circular corners (two levels when both ends share a column, as the list and the thread do, four otherwise): no scaled box, whose corners would stretch, and no animated `clip-path`, which runs on the main thread in exactly the frames after a commit. **Its spring is the picture flight's — `FLIGHT_SPRING` (ζ1 k1000, ~268ms, owner-tuned), as `FORUM_TRANSFORM_MS` / `FORUM_TRANSFORM_EASING` in `lib/forumContainer.ts` — both ways**, because a card opening into its picture, a row into its post or its picture and a card into its folder are one gesture. **The route's fade is started with the container** (`playUnderContainer`, `lib/routeCrossFadePlay.ts`): a press that records an origin also says which route change it rides (`expectRouteContainer`, `lib/pageTransit.ts`, read once by that change), and the fade is the ordinary fade through on the page's springs, on Web Animations so it runs on the compositor, started in the frame the container starts — the container lands on its spring a moment after the page has arrived around it. On the main thread the fade stood still through the long frames a route change starts with while the container went on. **And it waits for the page past the route's fallback**: a thread is a dynamic route, so the router commits the root `loading.tsx` first and the page a moment later, and cross-faded to that silhouette, the list had gone by the time the card arrived — the row was not carried, and the container came in from nothing over a placeholder that flashed up and was cut away. With a container expected, the list stays whole over the hidden page until the page replaces `data-route-fallback` (at most 600ms more, then it fades onto the fallback as any route does). The route is also warmed ahead, so usually there is no fallback at all: a row hover or press prefetches the thread's page as well as its layouts (`prefetchRoutePayload`, `lib/routePayload.ts` — the server's render of a client page, which reads nothing and counts no view). It hands off by fading the layer off the live card (`handoffFade`), as a folder's and a picture's do, so a picture or a count that changed under the layer cross-fades in. It shrinks from what is on screen of the card (a scrolled thread returns from its visible part), leaves a row the restored offset puts off screen — or in a concealed pane, the forum behind the gallery — to the route's fade, and lands at once on a scroll or a press. Standard tier only: under 减弱 the route's fade is the whole transition, under 关闭 it is a cut.

**A folder card opens into its page the same way, and the far end is the page, not its header.** Both lists of folders — /favorites' 收藏夹 and a profile's 收藏夹 — draw `FolderCard`, which names the card a press opens (`rememberFolderOrigin`, standard tier only; in the batch mode the link is inert under its checkbox, so a press toggles and records nothing). The page's column (`useFolderPage`, `data-folder-page`) is the container's far end — as much of it as is on screen, square, in the scroller's tone — and Back, Escape or 返回 shrinks it into its card again (`useFolderReturn`) while the restored offset leaves the card on screen; the route's fade otherwise. A card opens a screen — its name, its count, its first band of pictures — so the container ends as that screen: ending on the header squeezed a 4:3 cover into a 64px strip while the pictures came in on a clock of their own. The column's top must not move under the landing, so its header is final in the frame the page mounts — your own folder's from the cached folder list, a shared folder's seeded from the profile's public list (name and count) — and since the band's content may still change under the layer (a placeholder becoming pictures), the layer hands off by fading off the live page (`handoffFade`).

**A container in flight is turned, not dropped.** A route change removes the page a container is flying into. The flight outlives its owner by a microtask (React's development double invoke re-attaches the same element, which adopts it) and its page by two frames, in which the page coming in may claim it (`claimable`, `lib/routeContainerPlay.ts`) and turn it: Back pressed while a post or a folder is still opening sends the container back into its row or card, Forward while it closes sends it back out; unclaimed, it fades where it is. A turn leaves at once on the flight's curve, over the flight's reversal rule for what is left of the way — `0.35 + 0.65·√remaining` of a full leg, floored at 90ms (`turnContainer`, `HERO_REVERSE_*`) — from the leg's pose at the display's clock (`containerPose` at `performance.now()` less the leg's start): the frame a turn is claimed in comes after the incoming page's render, during which the compositor drew the leg on, and a pose read off the shutters put the container back where the main thread last saw it — the folder's landed full on screen, then jumped 58px down and 148px shorter. A turn keeps its clip when the new pair needs the same shape, as a leg sent back along its own pair always does.

**A container leg starts pending, and only presented frames show where it starts.** Every leg is given a current time one frame in, never a start time: the frame that brings the layer in has a page's copy to paint and raster — 64–129ms on the development server — and the display keeps the previous frame until it is ready; a pending leg starts on the compositor in the frame that is first drawn, which therefore shows its first pose (6.9% of the way). A start time fixed when the leg was built spent that wait: the folder's container was first drawn 45% of the way along. Per rAF, a pending leg reads its first pose for those frames and the main thread's clock stops through a long task while the compositor draws on, so the per-rAF sampler misreports both; read a container off the screencast instead (an injected rule that outlines its shutters in flat colours, `outline-offset: 0` so the line sits outside the clip it would otherwise be cut by, makes its box measurable in every presented frame). Measured that way at 1440 on a production build, the forum's leg follows the curve on the display: its first presented frame shows the first pose (7% of the way) and its top edge reached 30%, 60%, 79%, 89%, 95%, 98% and 100% in the frames after it; the history row's and the folder's are the same leg by construction (`lib/containerTransformPlay.ts`).

**A picture opened from a row with no shared image to fly is the forum's container transform, not a cut.** /history's rows carry `data-detail-origin={id}` and record the press (`rememberDetailOrigin`, standard tier only); the overlay grows out of that row and, closing, shrinks back into it while the row is on screen — on the flight spring and the flight's windows, timed by the picture's motion preference (`image`), as the flight is. Anything else that did not fly — a link in a comment, Forward, a row since deleted — arrives with a fade and the standard 16px (8px reduced) and leaves on DefaultEffects, as a large surface does. **The picture rides the container from its first frame**: the copy is taken as the overlay mounts, so the overlay has to mount complete — a row warms the picture's route (`prefetchRoutePayload`), its record and the still the detail shows first (`detailStillPreview`, decoded and held) on the intent ladder, and the record again at the click, beside the route's round trip rather than after it. Cold, the copy was an empty well that the picture popped into as the container landed. A picture still landing in the commit that mounts the overlay — in the memory cache, a task from showing, or a still that a quick press got ahead of — is kept in the copy rather than disarmed (`captureVisualClone`'s `keepLoading`), so it paints there the moment it lands, on the live picture's own fetch. **And that commit is the picture and its header, not the whole page**: the overlay's body (actions, tags, description, comments) waits for a later commit when a row's container will carry it in (`opensFromDetailOrigin`), as it does after a flight, mounts under the layer while the container runs and comes in with the handoff's fade. With the body in that commit, a 4× throttled phone mounted the overlay ~300ms late (890–980ms from the release, against 610–690ms before the warm); deferred, 670–740ms with the picture in the copy, and 100–134ms against 162–167ms on a desktop. **A copy stacks as one block at zero** (`place`, `lib/containerTransformPlay.ts`): the overlay's `z-detail-overlay` came along with its copy and painted it over the row arriving above it, so a picture closed back into its row never showed the row until the layer went. **And the copy carries the back affordance**, found by its class: the seal hides it with the overlay, and the marker the copy was searched by is one `captureVisualClone` strips, so it was missing from every copy and popped in at the handoff. Three rules came out of measuring it. **The exit is played when the close starts**, not when the overlay unmounts: a flightless close goes straight from `detail-idle` to `gallery-idle`, and the hero seals the overlay in that step while the router may still have a round trip to make — for all of it the bare list showed, then the copy popped back full size to shrink. `DetailOverlayTransit` plays it at that phase change, and the commit that later removes the overlay finds it played; when the commit comes first, the copy is taken in it. **The copy is what the overlay looked like before the seal**, so the seal's `visibility: hidden` is cleared on it (on the copy and its back affordance) — the fade path once copied it hidden, and every close that took it was a cut. **An entrance survives a remount of its own overlay**: the cleanup lands the run a task later and a remount in between adopts it, because React's development double-invoke of layout effects runs after the first paint of a transition render and restarted the container a quarter of the way in. `PicDetail` reads the picture off the overlay's own route id, so that layout effect needs no suppressed dependency — one suppression was enough for the React Compiler to skip the whole detail.

**/history is a sequence too** (`app/history/useHistorySequence.ts`): its rows name the list with `openFromSequence` on the press, walked in the screen's order — day group by day group (`readingOrder`), so the next picture is the row under this one — with the rows held for 撤销 left out. A row is the list's card: `reveal` turns the page through the screen's own state and waits for `[data-detail-origin]`, and the close grows back into that row when it is on screen. The turn is committed **in the close's own task** (`flushSync`, after a read the walk has already cached): a flightless close plays its exit at the hero's phase change, after the router's popstate, and must find the row where it will be. No `preview`: a row has no geometry, so a step reads the record.

**In-page tabs do not write history; the home pill writes it natively, the bottom-navigation way.** A tab row is local state and Back leaves the screen. /settings names its tab in the address (`?tab=account`, `settingsHref` in `app/settings/tabs.ts`) through `replaceState`, never a push, so a link or a reload opens that tab while Back still leaves the screen; a tab the visitor is not offered (账户 signed out) lands on one that is, and the address is corrected in place (decision 23). The home 图库/论坛 pill is the one exception — Android bottom navigation between the app's two top-level destinations (`lib/homeTabs.ts`): 图库 → 论坛 pushes one entry; 论坛 → 图库 goes back when the entry behind is this route's gallery and replaces otherwise, so switching never accumulates entries and from 论坛 Back returns to 图库 exactly once; a cold entry at `/?tab=forum` gets a gallery entry put beneath it; on `/` the drawer's 主页 / 论坛 rows switch through the pill (`requestHomeTab`) instead of navigating. Those writes are `history.pushState` / `replaceState` / `back`, never `router.push`: a tab is a query parameter on the route you are already on, and treating it as a navigation makes it an RSC request inside a transition that **races any other navigation started nearby** — and it was *deferred* by `TAB_PUSH_COALESCE_MS`, so it could land after the user had gone somewhere else and overwrite it. Measured over five runs, tapping 论坛 and opening a thread 200ms later gave three different answers — the thread opened once, the queued tab update clobbered it twice, and twice both navigations were lost, leaving the gallery pane on screen under a tab bar still reading 论坛; tapping 论坛 then 搜索 left the URL reading `/?tab=forum` while `/search` was the route rendering, and the route cross-fade never ran because the pathname it watches never changed. Cancelling the queued write when the tab bar unmounts does **not** fix it — the timer fires while the other navigation is still committing, so it wins the race anyway; removing the queue does. Next integrates native `pushState`/`replaceState` into its own router and syncs `usePathname` and `useSearchParams`, so the panes and the pill still see the change; nothing is started, so nothing can race, and a tab switch stops costing an RSC round trip. The coalescing window that once rationed pushes is gone: the model caps the stack at one entry above 图库 by construction, and a write waits for an open overlay's history entry to unwind (`settleHistoryLayers`) so it never reads that entry as the page. **It also waits for the tap's first frame.** Next renders a native history write synchronously, inside the task that makes it — written in the tap, the router's whole render stood between the tap and the first frame of the slide. `writeHomeTab` sends it once that frame is out (a frame, then a task: `afterFirstFrame`), at once if another input arrives first, so the next tap or key never acts on a stale URL, and a traversal before then cancels it. `npm run net:tabnav` is the regression check — it taps a tab, opens a thread after a configurable gap, and asserts where back lands.

**Tabs.** Render `TabPanes` / `TabPane`; do not wire this by hand. Panes are marked, never unmounted — `data-tab-pane="name"` plus `data-tab-pane-active` — and stack in one CSS grid cell so both can be on screen at once. Never gate a pane on `hidden` or `{cond && ...}`: the outgoing one has to survive the commit or there is nothing to slide out. Never put a `key` on `[data-tab-panel]`; that deletes the animation's own targets in the commit that starts it. Panes must be written in the same order as the tabs above them — direction is derived from DOM order. On the home page, call `startTabTransition` and then `writeHomeTab` on the tap: the transition is what the tap shows at once, and the URL follows once that frame is out (a back traversal lands a task later still, which is why the pill hands the panes its intent meanwhile); screens whose tabs live in local state need nothing beyond `TabPanes`, because the state update and the switch are the same commit. **A pane is a tab stop only while it is active and holds nothing focusable** (`tabIndex=0`, re-checked as its content changes): a pane with controls of its own is reached through them, and a tab stop on every pane put an empty focus ring around the whole panel before its first control.

**A pane off screen is concealed with `content-visibility: hidden`, not `display: none`.** Style and layout survive concealment, so showing a pane lays out the panel and reuses the pane's own; coming out of `display: none` it restyled and laid out its whole subtree inside the tap — on the production build the forum's 79ms, 1,119-element recalc, on dev 10ms of style and 10ms of layout for the one flag against 0.7 and 0.3 now. The rule (globals.css) is keyed on attributes alone and falls back to `display: none` where there is no content-visibility; so a script asking whether a pane is concealed reads the attributes (`isConcealedPane`), never geometry — which lays a skipped subtree out just to answer — and a lookup that must skip hidden panes asks whether every enclosing pane is active (`lib/hero/dom.ts`), since a concealed pane's cards still have client rects. A pane never shown has nothing to reuse, so each is **rendered ahead once** in an idle slice (`prerenderPane`): un-concealed in place, laid out and concealed again in one task, so no frame shows it and its first switch costs what every later one does.

**The driver is a class** (`TabPanesDriver`), for the one lifecycle a hook does not have. React moves `data-tab-pane-active` in its mutation phase, and from then the outgoing pane is concealed — any layout read before the driver's layout effect (a tab indicator measuring itself is enough) saw the page without it, and the browser clamped the offset: the header thrown down the screen with no animation to hide it. `getSnapshotBeforeUpdate` flags both panes before any DOM mutation of the commit, and reads the offset while it is still the reader's; `componentDidUpdate` runs the switch. The switch does its one forced layout first — `runTabTransition` writes the flags, reads (`panelTop`, the scroller, the pane heights on a panel-is-page screen), then writes (the offset, the animations), and nothing reads after a write.

**The whole pane moves by construction, and on a leaning panel the blocks on screen lean behind it — on the compositor.** `TabPanes lean` sets `data-tab-lean` on the panel. The tap and the reactive path read the same attribute, and both panes lean. These panels carry it:
- the home route's 图库 / 论坛 and its 全部 / 本站讨论;
- /policy, /messages, /settings, /favorites and /tasks;
- the profile tabs.

The admin console never leans: its main panels and the badge sub-tabs use plain `TabPanes`, because it is a work tool. **Its panel region is the strip's window** (`data-tab-window` on the box beside the rail): a section switch slides that region's width and is clipped to it. Sliding the scroller's width swept the leaving pane across the rail and out past the card, and brought the arriving one in over the page outside it; the sections' own sub-tabs find the same window. A tab panel in any other bounded region of a page marks that region the same way. `components/admin/registry.tsx` owns the offered tabs, role gates and lazy panels; its rail uses `activation="manual"`. `/admin?tab=` is replaced in place, never pushed (`app/admin/address.ts`), so Back leaves the console. A visited panel remains mounted; one not yet visited does not read. Panel headers use `SectionHeader`, whose fixed action row keeps titles aligned. A section's name and glyph are written once, in `components/admin/sections.ts`: the rail tab and `SectionHeader section=…` both read it. A read several panels show is one resource (`components/admin/sharedQueries.ts`): `admin_get_users` is `usersQuery` (`{ rows, stats }`), and `get_maintenance_status` is `siteStatusQuery`, read with the token and carrying the whole `document`. A panel's own shape of it is `useAdminSelect(resource, token, view)` with a `derived` view, whose identity follows the answer — `ConfigEditor` compares `initial` by reference. When an inline editor takes the run's last place from its row, the row's bottom corners move on the clip's clock (and back on the close's) instead of in the commits that insert and remove the panel. Inline editors use `InlineEditorPanel`: capture the pressed row with `captureInlineEditorLayout` before opening, and keep the editor through `onExitComplete`. Its reveal is two translated boxes — the section clips and paints nothing, its surface counter-translates — on Web Animations, never an animated `clip-path`; what follows the editor (and, closing at the end of a scrolled page, what precedes it) moves on a `transform` shared with `DataTable`'s presence (`components/admin/tableMotion.ts`), ending where one read of the layout with the panel collapsed says it will be. A development build's StrictMode remount adopts the running run — a cleanup lands it only a task later. It compiles whole; `scripts/testAdminInlineEditor.mjs` runs the compiled component and holds the press anchor (450px), the refused id (−139px) and the motion contract.

The look is the layered departure the GSAP lean had — the top leads, and each block on screen follows by a continuous function of its top — and the arrival has one too. **Each block eases after its pane: it is always where the strip was τ ago, τ being 15ms times its depth** (`lagSampler`, `LEAN_LAG_MS`). Depth is nothing at the panel's own top on screen (or the view's top, once the panel starts above it) rising to 1 one view height below, in 64px bands so cards that read as one row move together (`leanDepth`). A first-order lag is not a delay: the block moves from the first frame, at the pane's own speed reduced by τp′, and its lag is the strip's *speed* times τ, so it grows as the strip accelerates and dies as the strip stops — the shear builds over the first 40ms instead of tearing in one, and the page bends like a sheet rather than shearing like a rod. It cannot overshoot, so a block is never ahead of its pane, and it lands a moment after it, lower blocks last.

It replaced a spatial spring (ζ0.85, a period of 0–200ms by the block's top), which replaced a plain delay — each block where the strip had been 48ms ago at the bottom of the view (31%, which read as too much depth), then 24ms, then 10 and 15ms as the strip's own curve changed. The delay read dead and the spring that answered it read *odd*, and both readings were right for the same reason: a delay holds the bottom of the view still while the top leaves and then hands it the strip's full speed, and a spring built against the page stores momentum of its own, so the lower rows ran **2.3% of the window past their pane and back** — a wobble while the top was already still. Exponential smoothing is the smallest thing that has neither. Its peak is **11.9% of the window** at the bottom of the view, about 60px on a phone, reaching it 39ms in, and every block lands within 10ms of the strip.

The lag is time-based, so the lean is the same share of the travel at every width, and the sampler is solved rather than stepped: on a piecewise-linear input the recursion `position ← next − (pane speed · τ) + (position − here + pane speed · τ) · e^(−Δ/τ)` is exact at the sample points, which is what lets a block's keyframes be cut at a third of a pixel without the shape drifting (`lagSampler`). A turn needs no special case: `stripAt` reads the strip's whole recorded history, so a block behind the pane goes on the old way until the pane comes back past it — the lean reverses top first, as a wave.

Counting from the panel rather than the view is what makes it work under shared chrome. On the gallery's 全部 / 本站讨论, which sits under the banner, and on a profile's tabs, under the header, counting from the view's top set the whole visible panel off 12–20ms late: a slow start rather than a lean. The slope stays one view height to the longest lag, so a panel with little of itself on screen leans less rather than more steeply.

The mechanism is new, and each difference is a failure the old one had:

- **Every block is one Web Animations transform, set up once in the tap**, on top of its pane's: its lag behind the pane, worked out in the tap (`lagSampler`: the strip sampled once at 1ms for every depth, the recursion above stepped over that grid — 0.4ms a switch on a desktop, 4.4ms the first time), so a block lands on its pane when the lag has fallen to a third of the run's tolerance. No style is written per frame, nothing is laid out per frame, and no read follows a write — the GSAP lean wrote a transform to every marked block every frame (25–35 layouts and ~540 forced passes per run on the production build).
- **The blocks come from the structure, never from marks** (`leanPlan`): every child of the pane with a box, through wrappers without one; a bare wrapper — it paints, clips and contains nothing — taller than two bands, whose pieces on screen do not overlap, is taken apart, down to a list's rows and a grid's slots. A positioned stacking context such as a masonry slot is the block, never the card inside it, and a surface leans whole (/policy's card). Only blocks on screen lean, at most 32 a pane; everything else rides its pane's transform. So nothing can be left behind — the GSAP lean left the 全部 / 本站讨论 row standing because it had no mark — a component added later is a block or inside one without anybody marking it, and a block React replaces mid-run simply rides its pane, where the old lean animated detached nodes while the visible ones sat still.
- **A block costs two keyframes and a z-index.** Its keyframes are one pair — its pane's own place and the peak lag — with the lag's shape as a `linear()` easing that rises to 1 and falls back (negative after a turn), and every block of one delay copies one parsed model (`new KeyframeEffect(model)`); a keyframe per sample left twenty values a block to resolve inside the tap. For the run each block is stacked at z-index 1 (`relative` where it is static), written in the run's first frame rather than in the tap: an element whose transform animates may move anywhere, so the engine cannot fold anything painted after it into a layer beneath it, and with the blocks where they were, every row and card after the first leaning one, off screen too, came out a layer of its own (58 more at 1440). Stacked last, the extra layers are the blocks themselves.
- **A second tap turns every block from where it is.** A block behind its pane is *still* behind it while the pane comes back — the recursion is solved against the strip's whole recorded history (`StripSegment`), so a lower block goes on the old way until the pane passes it and the lean reverses top first, as a wave; no block starts over, and no pose is discontinuous at the turn.
- **A switch whose panes are still loading does not lean** (`loadingInView`). A block React replaces mid-run rides its pane from then on, so a skeleton giving way to its rows during the run jumped by its lag, and at the seam the two panes' lower blocks overlapped by the same amount.
  - So while a first-load placeholder is on screen in either pane, the whole switch slides as one plane. A placeholder counts if it is a `data-page-loading` root: the mark every first-load skeleton root carries for the footer hold.
  - A pane that reads when its tab is first selected therefore leans from its next visit on, once its rows are cached.
  - A new pane's first-load skeleton root needs the mark, or its first switch snaps.

**What the lean costs**, measured on dev against the same switch with the attribute removed: at 1440 the tap's task goes from 19 to 25ms and its first frame comes 3ms later, inside one frame's budget, with 74 → 89 layers mid-switch (twelve blocks). On a phone at 4× CPU the lean's own work in the tap is ~15ms (planning half, animations half) plus ~9ms of its blocks' first style resolution inside React's commit, and the first frame paints ~28ms more, because each block is a stacking context of its own for the run and its content is re-recorded: tap to first frame ~108 → ~137ms, 98 → 111 layers. A run gains one small layout — the restack, in its first frame — and the settle's one forced layout grows, since a block that loses its transform is a containing block no more (14 → ~117 objects, 0.6 → 1.3ms at 1440). The time to 10% of the travel and the speed through a reversal do not move (1440). **A press on a tab primes its switch, its release starts it, and the selection follows a frame later** (`primeTabTransition`, `playPrimedTabTransition`, `Tabs`). **A second tap sends the strip the other way at once, from where it is** (`turnStrip`). **Nothing animates the pane box, and the panel's clip is two style writes.** `[data-tab-panel]` used to have its `height` pinned to the outgoing pane's and tweened to the incoming one's over the same run as the slide, so the page's height changed with the motion rather than in one frame at the end of it. Both halves were wrong. The pin was a no-op dressed as a fix: the panel is a grid with both panes in one cell and both hold a box for the run, so its natural height already *is* the taller of the two — all the tween added was a forced shrink to the outgoing height at the start, which is the only reason it needed to clip at all. And what it bought is invisible: the one in-flow element below the panel on every `TabPanes` screen is the shell footer, and the footer carries `data-page-transit` for the tab transit — `opacity: 0`, `transition: none` — on the element itself (`registerPageFooter`, `lib/pageTransit.ts`): keyed from the scroller down to the footer, the rule walked the whole page for its invalidation set at every begin and end of a transit. After `endTransit()` runs in the same `onSettle` as the release, the footer returns on **DefaultEffects (~212ms at default speed), opacity only** — so it is never rendered at a pre-settle position and adds no second travel. On a screen whose panel is the page the content above does not move, because the pre-run clamp against the page's *final* scrollable height guarantees the offset survives the shrink; under shared chrome, the floor below does it.

**Under shared chrome the offset never moves, and neither may the height move it.** On a screen whose panel sits under a header — the gallery's 全部 / 本站讨论 under the banner, a profile — the switch leaves `scrollTop` where it was: the header stays put and the arriving pane starts at its own top. What could still move the page is its height: with the taller pane leaving layout at settle, the offset would sit past the new end and the browser would clamp it — the header thrown down the screen in one frame (本站讨论's height jump). So at settle the panel keeps a floor (`holdFloor`): a `min-height` of exactly the height that keeps the current offset reachable, blank below a short pane down to the bottom of the viewport — what a native tab page shows under a collapsed header. It is written whenever the viewport reaches the panel at all, so a pane whose content lands shorter after the switch cannot pull the page up either, and it is let go on the first scroll that no longer needs it.

The clip survives, because the outgoing pane is translated by `leavingOffsetY` — the difference between the two tabs' remembered offsets, which can be most of a screen — and without it that paints over the footer for the length of the run. It is `overflow-y`, not `overflow`: the panel *is* the centred `max-w-*` content column, and clipping both axes cropped the shared axis to the column for the whole run, so panes appeared and vanished at the text's own edge instead of sliding past the information area's. (On a wide screen the panes do pass through an edge now — but a soft one, up to 120px out from the column with its outer 96px faded, not a cut at the text: see the wide-screen window above.) The value is `clip` rather than `hidden` because `overflow-x: visible` beside `overflow-y: hidden` is *computed to `auto`* by the spec — which would quietly turn the panel into a horizontal scroll container — while `visible` beside `clip` is legal and leaves the x axis alone; the horizontal clip stays where it belongs, on the scroller's `[data-axis-running='x']` rule — or on a `[data-tab-window]`'s, for panes in a bounded region (the admin console above). It is written with the pane flags rather than after the measurements, so the run costs one style invalidation in the pointer handler instead of two.

`watchPaneGrowth` went with the tween: it was a `ResizeObserver` that re-tweened the same `height` for 2.5 seconds after settle, to absorb the case the tween could not — the height is measured at the moment of the switch, when a pane that fetches on selection still holds its skeleton, so the data landing a beat later moved the height again after the motion had visibly finished. A late change is now an ordinary reflow, like every other data arrival in the app, and `restoreAnchor` puts `overflow-anchor` back at settle so a shrink above the viewport is absorbed by scroll anchoring rather than by a tween on a layout property. Skeletons still have to be the right *length* (`PER_PAGE` rows, not eight) — that requirement got stricter, not looser.

Three CSS rules used to suppress `backdrop-filter` inside the moving subtree per transition; the blur is gone from the tone instead, so the cost is gone everywhere rather than suppressed in three places, and the legibility figure that justifies the plate (4.8:1 for a pure-white subject under black at 55%) never included the blur anyway. The tombstone is in globals.css where the rules were.

**Chrome that appears only on one side of the handoff is chrome that jumps.** The detail's zoom control uses `hover-reveal`: visible on compact and touch screens, hidden at rest only from `sm` on a device with a fine pointer and hover, and revealed by hover or keyboard focus. `HeroStage`'s landing target renders no children at all — so on touch the frame that hands the picture over conjured a 40dp button into its corner, and the frame that starts a close took it away. `html[data-image-hero-transition] :is([data-image-detail-zoom], [data-image-detail-step-arrow])` stands it and the step arrows down for the length of a flight. Anything else added inside the media box needs the same treatment or a twin on the Stage — **and every subject in such a rule names an attribute or a class of its own.** The arrows were matched as the step group's children, a bare `*`, which gives Blink nothing to narrow the invalidation by: the attribute's arrival on `<html>` at take-off, and its removal at landing, each restyled the whole document — 653 of a gallery's 763 elements, ~50ms on a phone at 4× CPU, the larger part of the wait before the picture moved. Measure a rule keyed on `<html>` by the element count of the restyle it causes (a trace's `UpdateLayoutTree`), not by its look.

**A viewport change mid-flight has to re-aim the container, not just the flyer.** `rebuild()` did only the flyer for a long time, and the window is a pose expressed against the *host's* box — so when the host resized, the same numbers landed somewhere else and the keyframes were still aimed at the old box. On a phone the trigger is the address bar collapsing. Measured at 400px wide with the flight 90ms in and the height changed by 60px: the flyer moved 19px in that frame while the mask jumped **682 → 784**, then converged on the pre-resize host and finished 8px short of the new one; `rebuildContainer` rebases it from wherever it is toward a freshly measured host, and after the fix the same test converges on 791 against a host of 792. Re-reading the host is safe because the overlay is the one node in that chain that never carries a transform — which is also why `createPlane` takes it as the plane's origin rather than the scroller, whose rect *is* the scaled box once a leg is running. A one-frame jump of a full-screen mask is the artefact that reads worse the lower the refresh rate, because it *is* the difference between two adjacent frames — do not reach for the frame rate when that is the complaint.

**A route with no media has to say so.** The handoff waits for a route with a paintable preview *and* a target, and only `DetailImage`/`DetailVideo` set either — so the failure branch could never satisfy it and the wait ran to `HERO_DETAIL_ROUTE_TIMEOUT_MS`: an image that turned out not to exist left the error page sealed and the screen blank for **30 seconds**. `resolvedWithoutMedia` on the registration is the terminal answer that lets the container transform finish and the error surface in its place; the closing path already fell back to a plain history collapse when `route.target` is null.

**上一张 / 下一张 step in place, inside the ladder — never a navigation** (decision 19, R10-001/002). The list a picture was opened from hands the viewer its order (`lib/imageSequence.ts`: `openFromSequence` on activation, `useImageNeighbours` in the detail); the viewer steps through it with the arrows, ←/→ and a horizontal swipe on the picture, loading the list's next page at an end, and a picture opened from nowhere has no pair at all. A step is **one tree**: the route never remounts and the router is never called. `imageHeroHistory.retarget` rewrites the ladder behind it — the guard steps back onto the base, the base's URL is replaced through the native `history.replaceState` path Next documents (the URL and `usePathname` change, the tree does not), and a fresh guard is pushed with the same token — so stepping never adds an entry, Back still closes the viewer to the list, and a reload names the picture on screen. The router route was the defect: `router.replace` dispatched inside a popstate listener landed before Next's own `ACTION_RESTORE`, which discarded it (the popstate waiters now resolve in a task after the dispatch, `afterDispatch`), and even without the race the flow overwrote the gallery entry and stranded a stale ladder forward of it.

**A Back can overtake the rewrite, and then the rewrite writes nothing.** Its own step onto the base is a traversal, and it writes only after that pop's dispatch (`afterDispatch`); a Back the browser takes meanwhile is resolved against the entry history is heading for — the provisional rung under the base — and on a busy main thread its commit queues behind the rewrite's, so Chromium dispatches both popstates before the rewrite's continuation (every time, measured). Written anyway, the base's new URL went onto the entry the Back had reached, the list's own: the list on screen under `/pic/<id>`, a guard pushed over it, a reload opening the picture again — the production build did exactly that. So `retarget` writes only if no pop has landed since its own and the base is still current, and a step the user's traversal abandoned passes its signal into the rewrite, not just into the wait (`replaceUnownedDetail`, for a viewer without a ladder, checks that the entry it replaces is still the picture's). A traversal that lands on that provisional rung from the base or the guard — ahead of the writes or after them, or a history-menu jump of two entries — is a close the router has already made: the list follows the viewer to the picture last on screen and the rung is stepped off, or the next Back would land on the list's own entry at the same URL and seem to do nothing. One thing no page can fix: a Back that names its target before the rewrite's step and lands after it — Playwright's `page.go_back()` reads the history and navigates by entry id, and `navigation.back()` resolves in the page — names the same entry as the rewrite, and the browser makes one traversal of the two; the Back is absorbed and the viewer stays on a consistent URL. A browser's own Back is resolved against the pending entry and never is, which is why the suite's Back is `history.back()`.

**The picture on screen is `useDetailStep`'s, not the route's `[id]` and not the URL.** The tree keeps naming the picture the viewer opened on, the URL lags the swap by a traversal, and the swap lags the press by the outgoing half of the transition; the location is adopted whenever no step is rewriting it (Back, a forward replay, a reload). Per-picture state resets on the swap, because the route no longer remounts to reset it. And the ladder's rewrite waits for the swap to land and an idle slice (`STEP_SYNC_IDLE_TIMEOUT_MS`), or runs at once when a close needs it: its two router commits re-render every location subscriber — the list under the viewer included — and beside the swap they delayed the outgoing half 200ms after the press (measured in development); a run of presses rewrites once, for the picture it stops on.

**The transition is M3's shared axis X, sequentially, in one tree.** `MaterialSharedAxis` fades the outgoing view out before the incoming one fades in, so no instant shows both and nothing needs a clone of the page: out on FastEffects sliding 30dp toward the side it leaves (8px reduced), one `flushSync` swap while nothing is visible, in on DefaultSpatial/DefaultEffects from the other side; off is a cut. The overlay scroller returns to the top on the swap. A swipe follows the finger (rubber-banded past an end), leaves at the speed it was released with, and docks back on the `layout` spring, at its release speed, when it does not page (`playStepReturn`); the picture's `touch-action` leaves `pan-x` out so the browser never claims the drag, while the pull-down and the pinch keep theirs. A video pages too, except from the band along its bottom edge, where its own controls take a horizontal drag for the timeline; it used to take no swipe at all, and the first video in a list stopped every swipe after it until an arrow was pressed. At an end whose next page is still loading, the drag rubber-bands as at a true end and a release that would page joins that read — one picture, once the page lands — rather than being refused for as long as the read takes. ←/→ step only while the viewer has the keyboard: no modifier, no IME composition, not in a field, not in a control that owns the arrows. **A second flick is never dropped for arriving mid-step** — a native pager takes a new drag from its in-flight pose, and so does this one (`takeOverStepMotion`): during the incoming half the travel stops where it is and becomes the drag's origin while the fade runs on; during the outgoing half the swap lands at once and the finger takes the incoming picture from its first pose. The recogniser does not wait for the step to settle (a flick timed off the URL would be lost in the incoming half). ←/→ and the arrows have no such gate: in the incoming half a press leaves from the in-flight pose, in the outgoing half it retargets the swap still ahead (the latest press wins). **A released drag always ends at rest.** Taking over the outgoing half lands its swap first, and that swap can unbind the recogniser — it did when the incoming picture was a video — whose teardown releases the drag; so the takeover checks the finger is still down before it pins a pose. Pinned without one, the picture stayed 30px off centre with nothing left to move it: "stuck halfway".

**The arrows are the dialog's trailing actions**, top-right opposite the back affordance and in its square tonal shape, pinned like it. Riding on the picture they moved with every box, so a run of presses chased the button across the screen; pinned, the pointer stays on it whatever the next picture's size. They stand down during a flight (the Stage draws none) and fade with the pull (`data-image-detail-chrome`). At a true end an arrow is unavailable, and it shows the wait while the next page loads.

**Closing after stepping lands on the picture on screen.** Before the return measures, `revealInImageSequence` asks the list to bring that card into view — turning its own page under the overlay if the steps crossed one (the list writes its address back once its own entry is current) — bounded by `STEP_REVEAL_TIMEOUT_MS`, after which the close is flightless rather than aimed off screen. A viewer opened without a flight reveals too, without waiting for it. **Focus follows the reveal, not the overlay's return.** The overlay's own return (`returnFocus`, the card in the active pane) can run before the list has turned its page — always in a flightless close — and then falls back to the card the viewer was opened from, which the page turn unmounts, leaving focus on the document. So once a reveal resolves and the detail has gone, the controller hands focus to the revealed card without scrolling (the reveal owns the position), and only while focus is lost: a choice made in the list meanwhile keeps it.

**A reload with the viewer open keeps the ladder in history with nothing behind it.** The page presentation's 返回 collapses it to the list it was opened from (`leaveImageHeroOrphanLadder` → `collapseOrphanMarker`, the entry the user came from with its search and page), and a cold entry with no ladder goes back through history when the entry behind is the app's, otherwise up to `/` in place — never a push (R10-003 / R4-041).

**Forward pressed during a closing flight is lost, and that is the browser's, not a bug to chase** (R10-010). The close collapses the ladder with one traversal; a `history.forward()` queued behind it is coalesced away by the browser before any popstate reaches the page, so there is nothing to replay. Forward after the close has landed replays the open as usual.

**The list's offset under the viewer is remembered per viewer entry** (the Navigation API's entry key), so leaving the viewer for another route and coming back with Back restores the list the router remounts under it (R12-017). It is read in an idle slice after a landing or a step, not at the landing itself — reading `scrollTop` there forced a layout of the whole page (77ms on a first open in development) — which is safe because the covered, inert list cannot move in between.

**Measure presented frames, not `requestAnimationFrame` gaps.** A composited animation keeps running while the main thread is busy, so rAF intervals report main-thread cadence, not what the display got. A CDP screencast (`Page.startScreencast`, `everyNthFrame: 1`) yields one event per presented frame; a full-viewport composited transform on an idle page is the control that proves the harness can reach 60. Headless and headed Chromium on the development machine both cap that control at 46fps, so presented frame rate needs a device with a real compositor; main-thread occupancy (`Performance.getMetrics`) is machine-independent. What holds from the 2026-10 measurements: **no single animated track is responsible for the flight's frame rate** (cancelling the corner morph, container mask, content fit and every shimmer together was worth about one frame per second), and removing the gallery's blurs was the one change that moved it. **The route's commit is about a fifth of the tap-to-landed cost, not most of it** — style recalculation is the same size and the rest is paint and raster — so **do not defer the route's chrome to `opening.landed`**: it saves little, splits the picture landing and the metadata into two events, and risks the header-height contract the handoff depends on. The time before the flyer moves is where `frameCache`'s press-path warming aims.

**From the tap to the detail being usable is what the user waits on, and the flight is a minority of it.** Measured on a production build in 2026-10 (fixtures, release of the press to each moment, medians of warm opens), after the flight curve, the restyle selector, the deferred `inert` and the body's publication were fixed: on a phone at 4× CPU the picture moves at +240ms, the detail settles (`detail-idle`) at +634ms and its body is on screen at +717ms — it was +300, +840 and later still; on a desktop at 1×, +89, +352 and +417. On the phone the gaps are the Stage's mount before the flight (~65ms: React renders it from the runtime store, `HeroStage`), building the flight (~40ms) and painting its first frame (~80ms); and after landing, the routed detail itself — its commit and its preview's decode can land after the flyer has, so a shorter flight waits on the route rather than finishing sooner — then the handoff's history guard and two frames. Probing it needs marks rather than guesses: `performance.mark` along the open path, read relative to the press's `pointerup`, beside a trace of the same open.
