# Motion system: springs, tiers and speeds

> Split out of `AGENTS.md`; read on demand. Cross-references such as "see Motion" or "under Shape" name sections listed in the index in `AGENTS.md`.

The rules. The per-component table is `docs/motion.md`.

## Motion

**There is one motion system and it is Material 3 Expressive's: every motion is a spring.** No call site picks a duration or a curve. The source of truth is `lib/spring.ts` (read its header before touching anything that moves); CSS gets generated `linear()` tables and settle times (`npm run motion:write`, verified by `npm run motion` inside `npm run check`), WAAPI gets `springTiming` / `surfaceArrival` / `surfaceDeparture` (`lib/springTiming.ts`), GSAP gets `spring` / `surfaceArrival` / `surfaceDeparture` (`lib/motion.ts`). The full standard, with the per-component table, is `docs/motion.md`.

### The springs

| Role | ζ | k | Settle | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `fastSpatial` | 0.6 | 800 | 348ms | a control moving: switch handle (travel, press swell and release — Compose snaps the swell, ours springs by owner decision), radio dot, slider handle, an icon turning; a menu, tooltip or toast scaling in **and out** |
| `defaultSpatial` | 0.8 | 380 | 412ms | a surface moving: tab indicator, a checkbox's mark drawing, a sheet / side panel / modal drawer arriving and settling after a drag (its overshoot lands on an edge skirt), the search view collapsing, a picture stepping in |
| `slowSpatial` | 0.8 | 200 | 569ms | the search view expanding; a detail arriving with no card to fly from |
| `fastEffects` | 1 | 3800 | 138ms | hover, focus, state layers; a colour going out; any small surface's opacity; a sheet / side panel / modal drawer leaving |
| `defaultEffects` | 1 | 1600 | 212ms | a colour coming in (tab, radio, checkbox box); a scrim; a dialog's fade; the button / icon button **press morph** (Compose: no bounce in a shape) |
| `slowEffects` | 1 | 800 | 300ms | a full-screen fade: a page arriving |
| `layout` | 1 | 700 | 321ms | bounds following a layout change: list placement, a box following its content, collapsibles, the docked drawer's width, the field label into its notch, a lightbox zoom step |

The six roles are Compose's `MotionScheme.expressive()` (`MaterialExpressiveTheme`'s default), verbatim; **where Compose has the component, the role is the one its source passes**, per property and per direction (`docs/motion.md` cites each). `layout` is critically damped like `spring(DampingRatioNoBouncy, StiffnessMediumLow)`, which Compose itself uses for `animateItem`, `animateContentSize` and `SizeTransform` — but at k700, not k400 (owner-tuned). Named component springs: the shared-element flight and container transforms ride `FLIGHT_SPRING` (ζ1 k1000, ~268ms, owner-tuned); **both pagers** — the tab strip and the lightbox — ride `PAGER_SPRING` (ζ1 k400, Compose's `HorizontalPager` snap); a determinate meter rides `PROGRESS_SPRING` (ζ1 k50, Compose's `ProgressAnimationSpec`, ~1.2s — a timer waiting for a meter to fill reads `COMPONENT_SPRING_MS.progress`). All three live in `COMPONENT_SPRINGS` and get generated CSS tokens. The theme wipe drives its radius on ζ0.8 k100, ending where it first covers the screen. Every spring settles at **1/512** of its travel (half an 8-bit step, ~0.5px on a typical move); the old 1% cut the soft end off every motion.

### How to choose

0. **Compose has the component?** Take the role its source passes (`MotionSchemeKeyTokens` in `androidx.compose.material3`), per property and per direction.
1. Moves or resizes → **spatial**; only fades or recolours → **effects** (never an opacity on an under-damped spring: it is clipped above 1). A shape morph (corner radius) is effects.
2. Bounds following a layout change (a slot, content height, another element's bounds) → **`layout`**, never a spatial role. A surface docked to a screen edge is *not* this: it takes its spatial role and an `edge-skirt-*` under the overshoot.
3. Otherwise by the size of the thing: control → fast, partial-screen surface → default, whole screen → slow.
4. **Leaving**: an opacity always on an effects spring. A transform that scales back into its anchor (menu, tooltip, toast) keeps its arrival's spatial spring; a surface leaving the screen (sheet, side panel, modal drawer) leaves on `fastEffects`. A colour fades in on `defaultEffects`, out on `fastEffects`. Everything starts on the frame after the press.
5. **Arriving**: transform on the spatial role, opacity on the effects spring of the same size (`surfaceArrival`), as two animations.
6. A follower that must wait for a leader (the arriving page) waits `HANDOFF_MS` (`--motion-base-handoff`, half a fast-effects fade), never a number of its own.

Under the **reduced** tier every spatial spring keeps its duration and takes the critically damped shape; the speed preference scales every settle time.

**The only non-spring curves** are `--ease-loop` (indeterminate indicators, which have no arrival) and `--ease-sweep` (skeleton shimmer), and the press **ripple**, which is Compose's `RippleAnimation` verbatim (75ms fade in, 225ms spread on `FastOutSlowIn` while the centre drifts to the middle, held while pressed, 150ms fade out after release *and* the spread) — as in Compose, where the ripple keeps its own tweens. The press itself is `data-pressed` (written by `RippleLayer`), which drives the M3E press morph on `Button` and `IconButton`.

### Three tiers and three speeds, with independent custom categories

The animation preference is **关闭 / 减弱 / 标准 / 自定义**, and independently a speed of **快速 / 默认 / 缓慢 / 自定义** and **入场动画**, one of **阻塞 / 不阻塞 / 关闭**. `lib/appearance.ts` owns all of it and writes `data-motion` / `data-motion-speed` / `data-entrance` onto `<html>`; `app/layout.tsx` puts them there from cookies at SSR and corrects them from localStorage before the first paint, so the tier is never wrong on a cold load and the CSS never has to wait for React.

**Custom effects and speeds share the ten categories in `lib/motionPreferences.ts`.** Each category has its own tier and speed; selecting a preset inside either dialog replaces all values on that axis only. The first custom choice inherits the current preset; later visits retain the edits. Both maps stay on this device, with validated named fields in storage, cookies and the pre-paint script. Read `motionTier(category)` / `useMotionTier(category)` and `motionScale(category)` / `useMotionScale(category)`; pass the same category to `scaledMs`, `springTiming` and `spring`. The default category is `feedback`. A gesture's fades, movement and delays use the same category. `motionMode()` is the overall selection, including `custom`; it is not an animation's tier.

**A disabled category must not stop another category.** CSS retains unscaled base durations, then derives `--motion-scale-<category>` from the speed and that category's enable flag. Feedback keeps `--motion-scale`; other categories must not multiply an already feedback-gated duration. GSAP's global clock carries feedback's speed only, with the category ratio on its spring or timeline, never twice. CSS spring surfaces use `data-motion-spring="overlay"`; their local clock and spatial shapes do not inherit into the controls inside. The three ordinary presets retain their established timing and reduced-motion behaviour. Progress indicators keep the existing documented exceptions.

**The two axes are orthogonal: the tier decides what kind of motion plays, the speed decides how long it takes.** 减弱 once carried its own 0.5 scale as well as stripping travel, so choosing "less motion" answered one question twice and the tier that most needed to feel calm instead felt broken. Speed applies to both tiers that animate; only 关闭 ignores it, because its length is zero.

`prefers-reduced-motion` reaches this **through** the attribute rather than around it: `system` is resolved in JS and `reduce` maps to **减弱, not 关闭**. What the preference asks for is less movement, and a user who wants none at all now has somewhere to say so. There is no 跟随系统 *option* in the control — the stored value is `system` until something is picked, and the select shows the tier that resolved, so a visitor whose OS asks for less motion sees 减弱动画 rather than a label that only says where the answer came from. The scripting-off floor is one blunt rule in a `<noscript><style>` in the layout.

**Every category uses one scale.** Durations are `calc(<base> * var(--motion-scale))` for feedback, or its category-specific counterpart; the speed contributes 0.7 / 1 / 1.4, and the effect contributes **0** for 关闭 or **1** otherwise. Two consequences:

- **The keyframe enumeration is gone** — a duration derived from the scale obeys the tier by construction. The invariant: *every `transition-duration` and `animation-duration` in the built CSS goes through `--motion-scale`* — greppable (a duration reached through `var(--transition-duration-*)` or `var(--duration-spring-*)` satisfies it), and the only exceptions are the four indeterminate progress indicators, each of which says why beside its value, plus the two third-party stylesheets.
- **The M3 duration scale is what 默认 declares.** 快速 and 缓慢 are an equal scaling of the whole system, so a scaled value is not itself a step on the 50ms grid — and that is the point. 0.7 and 1.4 are reciprocals, so the *ratios* between the parts of one gesture (a 100ms press against a 150ms state layer) are identical at all three speeds, which three separately-rounded duration tables could not have promised.

Scaling a spring's clock is legitimate where scaling one of its two halves would not be: normalised by settle time the curve depends only on ζ, so a uniform scale is the same spring at a different speed.

**A wall-clock timer that bounds an animation takes `MOTION_SPEED_SCALE.slow`, not the animation's own number and not the live speed.** Every duration in the CSS now stretches by up to 1.4, so a `setTimeout` written against the unscaled figure fires inside the motion it was meant to outlast: it unmounted `Modal` at 71% of its fade, dropped the splash overlay's full-screen `bg-surface` at roughly 0.6 opacity, and pushed both tab bars' routes ~200ms into a slide. The *maximum* rather than the current value, because the number bounds the animation and the speed can change between the two reads; holding an already-invisible node 40% longer costs nothing. The call sites that do this (`lib/overlay.ts`, `lib/routeCrossFade.ts`'s watchdog, `components/AppLayout.tsx`, `components/SplashScreen.tsx`, `components/FadeInImage.tsx`, `components/SliderCaptcha.tsx`, `app/admin/page.tsx`) are the pattern, and one more that needs it is a bug you will only see at 缓慢.

**入场动画 is the third axis, and it is a call-site decision rather than a token.** It answers a different question from the tier: the tier is about how much motion a gesture *you* asked for may use, and this is about whether the app volunteers any of its own — the grid cascade, `Reveal`, and the cold load's signature, the PicPony mark writing itself on. `entranceMotion()` reads `data-entrance` off `<html>`, and the invariant is *an animation is an entrance iff its call site reads `entranceMotion()`*.

**The signature is the one entrance with a mode, and the mode is what ends it** (`entranceMode()`). Both **阻塞** and **不阻塞** open a cold load on the full-screen splash (`components/SplashScreen.tsx`), the mark writing itself on in the middle of the screen. Under **不阻塞**, the default, the page ends it: the splash leaves as soon as the page has loaded — the document's `load` and a presented frame after it — mid-stroke if the mark is still writing, and never later than 阻塞 would, so a fast load shows little of the signature and a slow one most of it. Under **阻塞** the mark ends it: the page waits behind the splash until the signature has landed and held 200ms. **关闭** is every entrance off. The app bar's one-time draw-on that stood in for the splash for one release is gone; its hover trace stays. The splash keeps four rules, each a defect the earlier splash had (it waited for the shell to hydrate: 6–10s on a slow phone, for ever with scripting off):

- **It is in the first byte unless 关闭**: the server renders it from the cookie (anything but `entranceMotion=off`, at the standard tier), and the stylesheet paints it only while `<html>` agrees, so a stale cookie the pre-paint script overrules from storage renders it and never shows it. 减弱 and 关闭 play no Lottie, and a splash with nothing to animate is only a page held back, so neither shows it.
- **Its deadline is CSS** (`app-splash-lift`, delayed by `INTRO_LATEST_START_MS`, which the component writes into `--app-splash-deadline`): with no script in time it lifts by itself onto the page the server sent. A script in time takes it over (`data-held`) and leaves (`data-leaving`) on the page's load or the mark's completion, by mode; a takeover past the deadline leaves at once. `noscript` hides it outright.
- **The signature has the same 2.5s deadline**: past it the signature is not an arrival any more, so the splash does not start it.
- **Hit-testable while it covers** — a tap must not land on a page nobody can see — and not from the frame it starts to leave. Decorative, so `aria-hidden`.

The setting follows the account as two keys: the original front end's `introAnimationEnabled`, and this app's `introAnimationBlocking`; an account that says it plays without saying how leaves the device's mode alone (`lib/mascot/settings.ts`). Its cookie is mirrored from storage every time the settings sync starts (`cookie` on the entry), not only on a change: the first byte depends on it, and a cookie written by script lapses on its own (Safari keeps one seven days).

It shipped as a second CSS multiplier, `--motion-entrance`, and that had to come out, because **two keyframes serve both kinds at once.** `--animate-page-transition` is a cold mount's own fade *and* the route cross-fade's incoming half *and* `AuthModal`'s login/register pane swap; `--animate-fade-in` is a gallery skeleton appearing *and* the captcha's success state. Multiplying the token therefore suppressed a pane swap the user had just asked for, while `playRouteCrossFade` — which sets `animation: none` and drives the fade itself — ignored the preference on the path most navigations take; and `--animate-detail-arrive`, the substitute the two lower tiers get for the hero flight, turned a tapped thumbnail into a hard cut. A route transition, a pane swap, an overlay opening and status feedback are the *tier's* business; only motion nothing asked for is this switch's. What it gives up is two CSS mount fades that keep playing with the switch off (the gallery skeleton, the admin panel), both 400ms on opacity alone. The upload preview is no longer one: its arrival is `SizeSwap`'s swap (`app/upload/SizeSwap.tsx`), a response to the pick, and the tier's.

**减弱 is basic motion, not absent motion**, and that is the rule to check a new branch against:

> Keep the fades, the short travels, the state layers, the ripple, the indicator that slides. Drop the performance: the container-transform flight, a slide across the whole window, stagger, overshoot, decorative loops, Lottie playback.

Two readings of that rule: **"Drop Lottie playback" means every Lottie**, including the ones whose call sites argue they are a response rather than a performance — `LottieIcon` gated on `off` alone while its two call sites are a 3257×2148 login illustration filling 60% of a dialog and /search's 3000×1553 empty state, both behind a 60KB player, and `Logo`'s hover trace warmed that player on an idle callback; both are standard-only now. **And the skeleton shimmer is not a decorative loop**: it is the same class as the four indeterminate indicators, a band that says "this is a placeholder, not content", so it keeps looping under 减弱 and only `off` stops it. (The first version of the rule said *keep opacity and colour, drop travel, scale, rotation and stagger* — a fair description of 关闭, and it left 减弱 with nothing moving anywhere, because the CSS block it named re-declared `transition-property` without `transform`: the drawer did not slide, the switch handle did not travel, the tab indicator did not glide, a determinate meter's `scaleX` snapped. That block is `off`-only now.)

**8px is the weak form's travel, and it has to be the same 8px everywhere** (`TRAVEL_PX.small` in `lib/motionTokens.ts`, `--motion-travel-small` in CSS; the standard entrance travel is `TRAVEL_PX.standard`, 16px). `Reveal`, the grid, `Toast`, the route clone, the detail's own arrive keyframe, the floating back button, the swipe-dismiss and both shared axes (the tab strip, the detail's 上一张 / 下一张) all take it under 减弱; two of those last three were zeroing it, which made them the only things in the app that faded in with no travel at all on the tier whose whole point is that something still moves.

Overshoot is the one part a stylesheet can remove on its own, and it takes three declarations because nine springs share four `linear()` tables: `html[data-motion='reduced']` points the three under-damped shapes at the critically damped one, so a handle still travels and simply stops when it arrives; `spring()` in `lib/motion.ts` does the same substitution for GSAP. Everything else is per-component, because "what the basic form of this gesture is" is not a question CSS can answer: a cross-fade with the 8px shift where there was a full-window slide (the shared axis — the tab strip and the detail's 上一张 / 下一张 alike), a half-height rise where there was a full one (`Reveal`, the grid, `Toast`, the route clone), no cascade where there was one, and the hero flight replaced by the detail route's own fade — that last one on measurement rather than principle, since the flight is the most expensive thing the app does (36fps on a machine that idles at 60) and this tier's audience is a device that cannot afford it.

JS reads `motionTier()` — `prefersReducedMotion()` and `useReducedMotion()` are **deleted**, not aliased, because at more than twenty of their thirty call sites the "reduced" branch was a bare `return`: an absent animation rather than a weaker one, and an alias would have left every one of them quietly meaning 关闭 for a user who asked for less.

**关闭 has four documented exceptions**, and they are the answer to "why is something still moving": indeterminate progress (`Spinner`, `.m3-progress-*`, the top loader) slows rather than stopping, because a frozen ring claims the opposite of what it means; a determinate meter still shows its *value*, only the transition between values goes; a position the finger is holding is the input's own projection, not an animation — the drag itself is never gated, only the release; and a scroll offset is state, so every tier still lands on it and only the travel is dropped.

`no-motion:` is the call-site variant, and it replaces Tailwind's built-in `motion-reduce:`, which is a media query and therefore cannot see the app's own setting. Its five uses all do one job: standing a hover *end state* down, because the off tier drops `rotate`/`scale` from the transition list and what is left without a guard is the same 180° turn arriving in one frame. It matches `off` alone — it was `low-motion` and matched 减弱 too, which was right only while that tier stripped transforms. Unlike `dark`, it is built on `:is()` rather than `:where()` — it exists to beat the utility beside it, so it needs that specificity.

The `spring-*` utilities are covered by the transition rule automatically, because they set only a timing function and a duration and are always composed with a `transition-[…]` it already matches.

## `useGSAP`

`@gsap/react`'s `useGSAP` does **not** run your cleanup on a dependency change unless you pass `revertOnUpdate: true`. Without it, listeners, `Observer`s and `ScrollTrigger`s accumulate on every dep change. Pass it, or keep the changing value in a ref and shrink the dependency list.
