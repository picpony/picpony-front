**There is one tab control and it is `Tabs`.** The app once had four tab controls: three declared no ARIA roles at all, and the one that declared `role="tab"` implemented none of the contract that role promises — no arrow keys, no roving tab stop, no `aria-controls`. /tasks, which copied the structure (`useSlidingIndicator`, an absolutely-positioned indicator, a `data-tab` per button) before the primitive was reachable, reintroduced the defect `TabBar`'s own comment recorded as fixed: an active tab distinguished by colour with no weight contrast. `variant` covers all three shapes (`underline`, `pill`, `rail`) and `tone` covers /tasks' amber indicator. `activation="manual"` is for a row whose panels each start a read when shown (the admin rail): arrows move focus only, Enter or Space selects. A row that scrolls keeps the selected and the focused tab in view (its own scroll, never the page's) and fades at whichever edge hides more tabs; the underline indicator sits on the divider, covering it. A secondary tab's own shape rounds its **top corners only** (`rounded-t-md`), so the hover tint, the press wave and the focus ring are one object — a folder tab standing on that rule, its bottom edge flush against it. The radius sits on the *button*, not on the tint: the state layer's `::before` and the ripple host both take `border-radius: inherit`. The roving stop is rendered once — the server's markup and the hydrated tree have it on the selected tab — and moved afterwards in the layout effect that scrolls the selected tab into view, after that effect's read. Written by React during a switch, `tabindex` on the tab that had just taken the focus recalculated style synchronously in the middle of the commit's mutations (5–41 ms at 4× CPU on a development build). `scripts/testPresenceFocus.mjs` holds it to zero React writes per switch.

**A tooltip is not `title`, and an icon button no longer needs to ask.** `title` gives the OS font at the OS size with no token in it, a delay the page cannot set, nothing reachable on touch — and no `aria-describedby`, because `title` is a last-resort accessible *name* rather than a description, so a screen reader either read it in place of the label or ignored it. `IconButton` renders an M3 plain tooltip from its own `aria-label` (or `title`, or an explicit `tooltip`) and drops the native attribute, so there is only one bubble; anything else that wants one takes `useTooltip`. On touch a long press (500ms, cancelled by travel or a scroll) shows it, it stays Compose's 1.5s after the finger lifts, and the click that ends the long press is swallowed — except on a link, whose long press is already the platform's link menu; under a pointer the bubble is hoverable (WCAG 1.4.13) — leaving the control grants a short grace to move onto it.

`useTooltip` is a hook rather than a wrapper component on purpose: a wrapper must either clone its child — which breaks on any component that does not forward a ref — or introduce a box of its own, which changes layout. A control already owns its element and ref, so handing it props is simpler and layout-neutral.

The tooltip's first portal mount uses `@starting-style` for opacity zero. Its ordinary FastEffects transition then handles both directions from the current opacity; a transition without an initial style would animate dismissal but hard-cut every first appearance.

`title` is still right for one thing: a hint on content that *already shows its text* — a truncated tag name, a relative timestamp's absolute value — where the attribute supplements rather than names.

**`Modal`'s `footer` is one flex row, and it takes a leading member through `mr-auto`.** The row is `flex flex-wrap justify-end gap-3`, so the ordinary case is "spell the buttons in order and they land at the trailing edge". A member that belongs at the *leading* edge — /settings' 重新发送 beside 稍后 and 验证 — takes `mr-auto`, not a nested flex wrapper: an auto margin in a `justify-end` row absorbs the free space to its right. The row wraps as a guard — a fourth action or a longer label then takes a second line instead of being clipped by the panel, which is how the /block-groups row failed. A dialog with two branches gives `footer` a conditional rather than moving the row back into the body.

**Under a header, the 16dp between the headline and the content is 8 + 8** — the header's bottom padding and the body's top padding — not 12 + 4. A labelled field's floating label sits 6px above the field's box, and with 4px of scroller above it the first field of every editor dialog lost the top of its label's glyphs. The total did not move.

**Equal-width actions only grow on the row axis.** In a `flex-col sm:flex-row` action group, use `sm:flex-1` on the buttons and their skeletons. Unqualified `flex-1` changes the column's height basis to zero: Derpi's 56dp buttons collapsed to their 24px text line, while the empty skeletons collapsed to 0px. The column's default stretch already supplies full width. Derpi's actual pair uses `@lg:flex-row` / `@lg:flex-1` inside a query container: its two labels need 32rem of content width, and the tablet sidebar means viewport width cannot promise that.

**An overlay reverses from its current pose.** `Modal` commits its in-flight WAAPI pose before changing direction; `Sheet` initializes below the viewport only on its first placement, and a drag cancels the settling tween before taking over. Separate entrance/exit keyframes restarted a half-open panel from its full endpoint. A modal's scrim and its panel are siblings, each fading on the one clock, and the panel alone takes the transform: nested, their opacities multiplied and the content lagged behind its scrim. Standard travel is 16px, reduced travel 8px; a dialog arrives on `surfaceArrival` (transform on DefaultSpatial, opacity on DefaultEffects) and leaves on DefaultEffects, both scaled with the user's speed (see Motion) — it left over 200ms on the emphasized accelerate, which holds still for its first 80ms, so a 取消 seemed not to have been heard (see "Dismissals start on the next frame"). **There is one scrim at a time**: a dialog that opens while another is still leaving (确认 answered, and the run it started opening its progress dialog) takes the leaving one's scrim over at its current opacity, and the leaving panel finishes its exit one step above it — two scrims crossing for 130ms read as a flash of darker page.

**`Card interactive` renders a `<button>`.** A `<div>` with a cursor, a state layer and a ripple is a control no keyboard can reach and no screen reader can name — a prop that produces an inaccessible control is worse than an absent one.

**`Card as="a"` exists** so card-shaped `<Link>`s need not hand-roll the whole recipe: an anchor takes the same block/left-align/focus treatment a button does, it just never takes `type` or `disabled`.

`Avatar` has the matching escape hatch: `unoptimized` renders a native `<img referrerPolicy="no-referrer">` for a host that is neither in `next.config.ts`'s whitelist nor willing to serve a request carrying a `Referer` — do not grow a second avatar component for it. `priority` is for the one avatar that is a page's largest paint (a profile header): eager at high fetch priority, which is what Next 16 replaced its own `priority` with.

Its `size` is a union — `32 | 40 | 48 | 56 | 'hero'` — rather than `number | string`, so the ladder is a constraint. `'hero'` is for the one avatar that changes size at a breakpoint and cannot be sized inline at all (an inline `width` beats any class); the 96 → 128 pair lives in the component.

**Four primitives stand a default down when the call site names its own** — `Skeleton` for a radius, `Badge` for `max-w`, `ProgressBar` and `Field` for `w-full`. `Field` only stands down for an unconditional `w-*`; a breakpoint width such as `sm:w-32` keeps the full-width mobile default. The pattern matters because `cn` is a plain join: emitting both leaves Tailwind's output order to pick, which is not a decision anyone made.

**`Select`'s trigger is as wide as its widest option, not its current one** — a combobox that resizes when you pick a value re-lays-out the row under the pointer that just chose it (/settings' content filter, options 完全安全 (Safe) and 中等限制 (Spoilers), did exactly that). Every label is rendered into one grid cell with all but the selected one `invisible` and `aria-hidden`, so the browser's own intrinsic sizing takes the max and it stays true when the options change. No measurement, no `min-w` to remember. With a `label` it is a form slot — the outlined `.m3-field` shell a labelled `Input` wears, the label floating into the notch at 56dp — so a form with a dropdown in it is one label system rather than a notched field beside a captioned filled box.

**`ToggleSwitch`'s press comes from pointer events, not `:active`.** On a touch screen the browser owns `:active` — Chrome delays applying it and holds it for a minimum period afterwards, so the handle could still sit at its pressed width after finishing its travel. Off a real `pointerup` the shrink and the travel start in the same frame and, both being `FastSpatial`, land together; `pointercancel` and `pointerleave` are part of it, or a press that turns into a scroll leaves the handle swollen. Under a finger the press waits for the tap timeout like every other press (below), so scrolling a settings list past a switch neither swells its handle nor pulses it.

`Spinner`'s colour is one `tone` axis — `primary` / `on-primary` / `inherit`. `primary` resolves to **primary-ink**, a foreground over a secondary-container track, not the pale primary fill. Built-in foregrounds now clear 4.5:1 on that track; exact custom themes retain their established ink policy.

Press feedback is `data-ripple` plus the `state-layer` utility; there is no `usePressable` hook — nothing in the repo defines one. Press lives in `spawnRipple` (`lib/ripple.ts`) and the `state-layer` utility in `globals.css`, and `Button`/`IconButton` already carry both. **A finger's press waits for the tap timeout**: `trackPress` holds a touch's wave for Android's 100ms, plays it at once on a quick tap, and lets it go when the touch travels past the slop or becomes a scroll — a wave on every `pointerdown` flashed under every flick of a list; mouse and pen stay immediate. A control whose hit area is larger than what it paints carries `data-ripple="inner"` and paints its wave into a `[data-ripple-host]` first child, so its own box does not clip its target (see the touch-target note below).

**There is no scroll-driven reveal, and that is a decision rather than a gap.** `useScrollReveal` in `lib/motion.ts` is gone: it never had a call site, because an entrance cascade is for *picture* content — the gallery already has one in `useStaggerGridOn`, ordered by visual position rather than DOM order — while on text it makes rows the reader came for behave like an animation. It was also the only ScrollTrigger consumer, and `lib/motion.ts` registers its plugins at module scope, so the plugin shipped in the root chunk of all twenty routes to serve nothing.

`<Reveal>` is therefore the app's one general entrance helper, and it plays on mount — a *client* mount: an instance hydrating server HTML stays still, since that block has been on screen since the first paint and an entrance would hide it and fade it back (the maintenance screen is the case). A below-the-fold reveal, if ever genuinely wanted, does not need ScrollTrigger — an `IntersectionObserver` rooted on the app scroller is a dozen lines, and `PicDetail` already has two. Four constraints carry over:

- **Never above a gallery card** — it parks targets at a `y` offset and the hero flight reads `getBoundingClientRect` on press.
- **Never inside a leaning pane** (`TabPanes lean` — the home route, /policy, /messages, /settings, /favorites, /tasks and the profile tabs) — a block on screen there takes a transform animation of its own for the run, and a reveal would move the same node on a second clock.
- **Never inside any tab pane whose content swaps** — /user/[id], /messages, /favorites, the home route: the pane transition and the reveal would animate the same nodes on two different clocks. `StatusView` asks `useInTabPane()` (`components/TabPanes.tsx`) and knows on its first render; it used to find the pane in a layout effect, after a `Reveal` had already mounted and started.
- **Never over content that already cascades on mount** — a second entrance on top of an existing cascade is the "动画重叠" failure, not extra polish. /tasks uses `TabPanes lean` with no separate row cascade. /admin's panels instead use plain `TabPanes`, with no extra entrance on their contents.

**An interactive element may not be nested inside another one.** A `<button>` inside an `<a>` is invalid HTML, and it fails invisibly: the gallery card's spoiler cover was a click-handled `<div>` inside the card's `<Link>`, so the only focusable node was the link — Tab landed on it and Enter navigated to the picture the cover exists to hide. Lift the second control out to be a *sibling* of the link and position it over the top. Where the enclosing element is genuinely the convenience target (the image detail's media box, which zooms on click), keep the container's handler as a pointer affordance and put a real control inside it, so the pointer path and the keyboard path both exist. `DropZone` is built that way: its click and keyboard target is a sibling button laid under the zone's content, which passes the pointer through except on controls of its own — so /upload's 移除文件 is a separate, reachable button rather than a flattened child of a `role="button"`. A drop is checked against its `accept` — a mismatch goes to `onReject`, or, without one, on to `onFile` so the caller's own validation says why rather than the file vanishing — and a drag of a known wrong type shows as refused instead of inviting the drop.

**One hidden control is pointer-only on purpose: /about's wordmark.** Ten quick presses open the developer guide (`DeveloperGuideModal`); there is no keyboard twin and no visible entry. **Back closes the topmost overlay, and nothing under it** (owner decision 1). Four rules make it safe, and each is a bug the first version had: **a pop between a layer entry and the entry beneath it is consumed** (same URL, same tree: Next's `ACTION_RESTORE` for it would discard a pending navigation, the R10-001 hazard), by a capture listener and by the hero driver's own first line; **closing any other way steps back off the entry**, so history never keeps a dead entry that makes the next Back do nothing; **a navigation from inside a layer supersedes the layers** — the write is held, the entries unwound, the layers asked to close, and the write replayed on the page's entry, so "confirm, then navigate" leaves `[page][new page]` with no call-site code; and **a stale layer entry** (reload, restored tab, Forward onto a closed dialog) is stepped off silently. A surface that refuses to be dismissed (Esc off, an upload running) swallows Back rather than obeying it.

**A gesture never outlives the surface's state.** The phone drawer's swipe (`useDrawerSwipe`) writes inline styles for as long as a finger or its release owns the panel, and every ending hands them back: a release; a change made elsewhere meanwhile — Back (on a phone the edge Back gesture reaches the page as a drag on the open drawer before the system takes it), Esc, a row, a route change — after which the panel springs from where it is to React's state and the release finds nothing to do; `enabled` flipping and unmount, whose cleanup runs after `context.revert()` and clears what the revert leaves (GSAP's resting `translate3d(0,0,0)` with `translate: none` outranks the class). `EmptyState` and `ErrorRetry` are two presets over one `StatusView`, so a list that is empty and a list that failed have one silhouette. Both take `size="page" | "pane" | "inline"` — match the enclosure, because a half-viewport block inside a 120px well makes the well scroll.

**`fill` drops `page`'s `min-h-[50dvh]` floor**, and that is not tidying — a floor beside `flex-1` is a competitor, not a backstop. `[data-page-content]` has two in-flow children, the content wrapper *and* the footer (≈190–240px), so `flex-1` divides the space **above the footer**; once that space falls under 50dvh the floor overflows the column and the block lands at the top of the scroller. **"Complete" is not "loaded".** `HTMLImageElement.complete` is true for an image that has **failed**, so a `complete` check alone marks a dead image as ready — `FadeInImage` removed its shimmer and left a blank card. The predicate is `complete && naturalWidth > 0`.

And it has to be **assigned, not raised**: the check runs on every change of the layered loader's `displaySrc` and must clear as well as set, or a card that showed anything once never shimmers again and returning to a page whose images had already failed once brings the cards back bare. `npm run perf:pageturn` asserts that count stays zero — every card in view must be showing a shimmer or the give-up plate.

**Loading is the destination's own shape, not a spinner.** A list loads as `Skeleton` rows in the row's own geometry, a grid as `ImageGridSkeleton`, a card as a card. A `Spinner` is for an action in flight — a submit button, an upload, an inline "querying…" — not for a page: a centred dot says "something is happening somewhere" and then reflows the whole screen when the content lands. The test is whether there is a destination shape *yet*, and the shape has to actually match: a placeholder whose spacing or thumbnail differs from the card it stands in re-spaces and shifts the list the moment content lands — the one thing a skeleton exists to prevent. `.skeleton` sits in `@layer components` in globals.css, and that is load-bearing: unlayered, it beat every utility, so a call site's `absolute inset-0` never applied and every positioned shimmer in the app — each gallery card, the banner, the detail's media well — measured 0×0.

**The route fallback is the page column's, never the picture slot's.** Next gives every parallel slot of a layout that segment's `loading.tsx` (the loading data is stored on the parent segment and applied to each of its slots), so `app/loading.tsx` is also the `@imageDetail` slot's fallback — and that slot is rendered in the content host beside the scroller, not in the page column. **A name still being asked for is a placeholder in the name's line, not the raw value renamed a moment later.** `useTagNames` answers a name, `null` (show the tag itself) or `undefined` (still asking, at most 1.2s); a caller holds the slot with a `Skeleton` in that line's type role and fades the answer in (`NameFade`), never on mount. **A removal decides where its focus goes, in the commit that removes the entry, on every tier — and `PresenceList` does it.** It is read before the commit, while the leaving entries are still in the DOM (under 关闭 the commit removes them outright). **A card lays out itself, not the page.** Each `.masonry-item` carries its own height — `--mr`, the card's aspect, which the placement already stacks by — and size and layout containment, and `.masonry-grid` is contained the same way at the placement's height, so a picture decoding, a plate, a spoiler cover or the next chunk of a page lays out one slot or the grid and stops there. **There are two text fields, and the label decides which.** They share the tone vocabulary, 56dp form height and `.m3-field` shell. The outlined field takes a 20dp corner (`--radius-xl`, against the token files' `CornerExtraSmall` for a text field); the filled field takes an 8dp corner, matching `Select`'s default field shape. The search field is the separate pill shape (`shape="pill"`, implied by `size="lg"`) — one corner for every search box, at 56dp and at the dense 40dp step alike. The softer filled boundary is a deliberate project refinement, not a new M3 token claim.

A **labelled** field is a slot in a form: it has a name that has to survive being filled, and it gets M3's *outlined* field with the label floating into the outline. That is the whole point of the pattern: an empty field and a filled one stop being different objects — with a stacked label, a form of six empty fields is six blank boxes with captions floating between them, the caption belonging to the box below as close as the one above.

An **unlabelled** field is not a form slot — a search box, an admin filter, a chat composer, a thing you type into and act on immediately, whose placeholder is its whole identity. It gets the *filled* treatment: `surface-container-highest`, no border, no shadow; dressed as an outlined field it read as a form control whose label had failed to load, and it put the heaviest boundary around the least ceremonial thing on screen. The tone matches `Select`'s trigger and the component container table below. An unselected filter chip takes `surface-container-high`; it is a different control role, not an alternative field tone.

The notch is a real `<fieldset>`/`<legend>` pair, not a label painted over the border with a matching background: an M3 outlined field has no fill, so there is no colour to paint with, and a `<legend>` is the only thing in CSS that removes a section of a border. The `<label>` the user reads and each outline's invisible `<legend>` therefore carry the same words; the copies only supply the right width. They stay in step because the legend's font-size is exactly 0.75x the label's and the label scales to 0.75 as it floats, 0.75 of `body-l` being `body-s`. `FieldLabel` supplies the same label and outlines to `Input`, `Textarea` and labelled `Select`. Geometry lives in `.m3-field` in globals.css, because it turns on `:focus-within` and `:placeholder-shown` matching against a *sibling*.

The label and legend explicitly own their font weight and tracking as well as their size; the legend's tracking is also 0.75x, so surrounding emphasized text cannot change the notch. Label travel and notch opening share FastSpatial; label ink, button materials and selection-control colours use FastEffects. Field boundaries and focus ink use FastEffects too — the clock of every focus indicator in the app (native inputs, `CodeInput`, the rich-text editor, buttons, chips, rows, links); what keeps focusing and blurring continuous is the fade over the resting outline, not the length. `transition-ui` carries the same pair (ink on FastEffects, travel on FastSpatial), and the state layer keeps its own 150ms response, which a photograph's hover veil (`.media-hover-scrim`) and a filled field's hover share.

The `<fieldset>`'s negative top inset exists to cancel the UA drawing the top border through the vertical centre of the `<legend>`, so it must be zeroed when there is no legend — left in, an unlabelled 44dp field painted its line 6px above its own box. When a field's geometry looks *slightly* wrong, measure the painted line, not the element's rect.

**Focus follows the field's boundary.** The outlined field has no outer ring: its own outline thickens to 2px in `primary-ink`, avoiding two nested boundaries. A fixed 2px outline fades over the resting 1px outline in the same place, with the same notch; changing `border-width` itself snapped between device pixels. The filled field has no outline to nest inside, so it takes the ordinary `focus` ring, faded as a whole on its fieldset's pseudo-element. Both use FastEffects, reverse from their current opacity on a quick focus/blur, and respect the motion tier and speed. Both key off `:focus-within`, because the element wearing the indicator reports on the control inside it. An invalid outlined field retains its error outline and label; on a filled field, the focus ring takes precedence over the error ring.

**An unlabelled field can carry its own actions.** `trailing` puts controls inside the box — /search's clear control (while there is text), 以图搜图 and the submit live there. `Input` has **one height for a form slot and one for chrome**. `size="sm"` is the other axis — 40dp with `body-m`, for a field that is *chrome*: an admin filter, a control in an `.m3-row`. It shares the 40dp height, 8dp corner and 16dp text inset with `Select size="sm"`; the dropdown uses `label-l` because its value is a control label, while typed text stays `body-m`. Both are 14px. **Busy is active, disabled is unavailable.** A loading `Button` / `IconButton` keeps its material, ink and exact footprint while its spinner (or `busyIcon`, e.g. `LoadMoreButton`'s dots) reports activity — and keeps its focus: it is `aria-disabled` + `aria-busy` with activation blocked (`blockActivation`: no default action, so a busy submit does not submit again, and no bubbling), **not** native `disabled`, which drops a focused element's focus onto the body (measured in Edge — a keyboard user's next Enter then did nothing). Native `disabled` is for an actually unavailable control, and only that takes the 0.38 treatment. A control a caller marks both `disabled` and `loading` is busy: the primitives emit native `disabled` only for `disabled && !loading`, so a submit that disables itself while its request runs keeps its focus. Icons and text must not shift when busy begins, and an inherited spinner takes the control's own ink, including on media.

**There is no outlined button**, and M3 does specify one. All four of its uses were a secondary action beside a filled primary one (取消 next to 保存, 重置 next to 检索), and at that job a 1dp keyline reads as a button that lost its fill rather than as a quieter button; `tonal` is the step M3 puts directly below `filled` for exactly this pairing, and it separates from the surface the way everything else here does, by a container tone rather than by an edge. Removed rather than left unused, because a variant that exists gets reached for. The same reasoning retired the filled field's border and the filter chip's: this app separates by tone.

`IconButton` keeps its `outlined` variant, and that is not an inconsistency — it is the one "switch that is currently off", which is what M3's outlined icon button means.
