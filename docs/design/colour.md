# Colour

> Split out of `AGENTS.md`; read on demand. Cross-references such as "see Motion" or "under Shape" name sections listed in the index in `AGENTS.md`.

Tokens, the eleven palettes, 单色/多色, alpha rules, focus rings, forced colors.

Only `--md-sys-color-*` tokens, via their Tailwind utilities (`bg-surface`, `text-on-surface-variant`, `border-outline-variant`, …). No raw hex, no `rgb()`, no Tailwind palette classes (`bg-slate-800`, `text-red-500`).

A `dark:` variant is almost always a bug: the token already flips. The legitimate uses are swapping a whole asset (`Logo.tsx`) and nothing else. Reach for the container/on-container pair instead of hand-picking a second tint.

Pair only within a role — `primary`/`on-primary`, `surface-container`/`on-surface`. Dividers use `outline-variant`; text-field borders use `outline`.

**`scripts/palette.mjs` generates every tonal value, and it is the authority.** Run `npm run colors` for the diff, the contrast table and the ten-theme matrix, `npm run colors:write` to write. Three products: the **default** theme's declarations are substituted in place in `globals.css` — it only touches `--md-sys-color-<token>: #hex;` lines, so the ~40 paragraphs of reasoning between them survive — while the nine character themes are written wholesale to `app/theme-palettes.css` and their brand colours to `lib/generated/themeColors.ts`. Both generated files are also *checked* on a plain run, so a stale one is a failure rather than a silent disagreement. It is **idempotent** against the current file, and it exits non-zero rather than writing if a tone lands off target, the neutral-variant palette's chroma moves, or any theme drifts from the default on any measured pair.

The basis is **HCT**, because that is the space M3 quotes its own numbers in: "neutral chroma 6" means 6 in HCT, and no single OKLCH chroma means the same thing at every lightness. Role → tone is verbatim from AOSP's `ColorLightTokens.kt` / `ColorDarkTokens.kt` (v0_210). `accent-*` stays in OKLCH and that is not an inconsistency — an even hue sweep at fixed lightness and chroma is the entire point of that scale, and OKLCH is where that relationship is expressible.

## The palette axis: eleven themes, two 配色方案, and `primary` is decided with the artwork beside it

`data-palette` on `<html>` selects one of ten built-in themes or the user's own; `data-palette-hues='mono'` selects **单色**, and its absence is **多色**, the default (decision 27). A built-in theme's `primary` is **the character's own hue from the MLP-VectorClub colour guide, at a tone and a chroma chosen for the largest area on screen** — a refinement made during the palette review, recorded per theme with its guide row in `THEMES` in `scripts/palette.mjs`. Everything else is shared: the role→tone map, the harmony (M3's own, see below), the semantic ramps, and the three things still solved from the two fills.

**Direction A governs built-in fills and new image recommendations (decisions 33–34).**

- Keep the character's established hue and choose its appropriate register. The light tones are Applejack 86, Fluttershy 92, Lyra 88, Rainbow 84, Twilight/Pinkie 82; Chrysalis 42, Luna 34 and Rarity 39 stay deep. The brand is held to the same rule: the mascot's mane hue stepped 9.7° toward coral (H5.5) at the warm cap T86, `#fdcad3 / #fdaebe` — at its own hue (355.7) every in-register tone fell within ΔE 4.7 of 碧琪.
- `lib/clearColor.ts` is the shared chroma rule: light = min(C36, 0.96 × available gamut); deep = min(C48, 0.72 × available gamut). A pale peach's modest absolute chroma is not a defect. Built-in dark fills use the same hue/register rule seven tones lower.
- Role harmony remains the character's existing secondary/accent hues, bounded chroma and M3 neutral ladders. Semantic colours and tag categories do not follow the theme. 多色 / 单色, borderless theme tiles and one custom companion hue remain the contract.
- Built-in foregrounds are solved for text contrast. Exact manual and saved custom themes retain their full existing role derivation; recommendation generation is never run on saved values.

### The harmony is M3's, verbatim — that is 单色

```
secondaryPalette:      hue,        chroma 16
neutralPalette:        hue,        chroma  6
neutralVariantPalette: hue,        chroma  8
```

单色 is `SchemeTonalSpot` with one change: **its tertiary roles read the secondary palette**, so no second hue appears anywhere (TonalSpot's own tertiary, hue + 60° at chroma 24, is not used). Before 多色 nothing read tertiary, so 单色 renders as the app always did; the three accent moments (below) read the secondary pair under it.

These numbers were once offsets reverse-engineered out of globals.css — `#755360`, `#894d1f`, `#7f7378` — kept because changing them would move the default theme, not because they were correct: measured against the spec they were secondary chroma **19.4** (21% high), tertiary **+57.31° chroma 37.1** (55% high) and neutralVariant **−9.48° chroma 6.7**, with only `neutral` already exact. Switching cost **18 declarations** in the default theme (`outline`, `on-surface-variant` and `outline-variant` among them, the two most repeated non-brand colours in the app); the measured pairs did not move (`outline`/`surface` is still 4.27:1 light and 5.83:1 dark). Paid once.

**The chroma stays flat even though `primary`'s is not.** M3 pairs a constant secondary 16 with a constant primary 36 — a ratio of 0.44 — while this app's primary ramp runs **45.7–56.8**, so the ratio here lands between **0.282** (the brand and four characters, 56.8) and **0.350** (邪茧, 45.7). Making secondary proportional would restore the ratio and would *not* be M3: the spec's number is a constant, and a focus ring that gets louder on the more saturated themes is a focus ring answering a question about the brand rather than about the keyboard.

**ASSERTION 8 is what makes any of this checkable.** ASSERTION 2 measures emitted chroma against *this repo's own* constant, so it is self-referential — a dependency bump that retuned TonalSpot would have passed every check in the file. ASSERTION 8 builds a real `SchemeTonalSpot` per theme and compares the three palettes 单色 reads at every tone the role map uses, and asserts that 单色's tertiary, focus and chip roles are its secondary ones; `primary` is excluded, because the library pins it flat at 36 and this app's is `rampChroma`. It passes today for all ten, and it fires on a **one-unit** chroma drift.

### 多色: the character's second colour and accent

多色 fills two slots M3 leaves to the scheme with the character's own colours, both read off the same guide rows as the primary (every one cited in `THEMES`; the default's off the mascot renders):

| role family | 多色 | read by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `secondary*` | the **second colour**, chroma 14–18: broad and quiet | every selection container — the drawer's current row, the tab pill and rail, a selected or tonal icon button, a tonal button, the current contact, an unread row, your own chat bubble, the listbox's chosen option — and every track (progress, slider, spinner). They follow for free |
| `tertiary*` | the **accent**, chroma 36–48: a mark | three **accent moments** only: a favourite or like that is on (`IconButton selectedTone="tertiary"`: the tertiary container pair), the level meter (`ProgressBar tone="tertiary"`: tertiary on its own container), an empty state's glyph (`EmptyState`, through `StatusView`'s `iconTone`) |
| neutrals | the default theme only: **the mascot's coat** — cream in light (H48), periwinkle-lavender in dark (H285), M3's chroma 6 / 8 | every surface |
| `focus`, `chip-selected` | **TonalSpot's secondary, in both 配色方案** | the focus ring answers "where is the keyboard", and switching the scheme must not move it; a selected filter chip keeps the single-hue pair because the tag categories own chip-shaped colour at tone 90 — **an accent never wears a chip** |

The default's pair is the mascot's own: her mane pink (`#e06c9f`) is the brand's ramp seed, her coat shading periwinkle-lavender (the second colour, H272), her bows periwinkle (the accent, H268 — about 1% of her pixels, which is what an accent is), and her coat cream the page. The nine: 苹果嘉儿 leaf, 小蝶 rose and butterfly teal, 天琴 apricot and eye orange (her harp gold is the warning hue), 邪茧 mane blue and eye lime, 云宝 mane violet and orange, 露娜 eye teal and mane violet, 瑞瑞 diamond blue, 暮光 mane indigo and cutie-mark magenta, 碧琪 balloon blue.

**Second colours may coincide; a theme is told apart by its primary and its accent.** 默认 and 暮光 share the mascot's periwinkle by construction, 邪茧's mane blue sits beside 瑞瑞's diamond blue, and 碧琪's balloon blue is near both. Each is cited from its guide row, and moving one off its source to separate a pair visible only side by side would trade a documented colour for a guessed one (D1-023).

Four rules keep it from being noise, each an assertion. **The second colour is ≥ 40° from the primary** (ASSERTION 10): at tone 90 a hue under 40° away does not show at all — the mascot's mauve and lilac, 16° and 39° from her pink, rendered exactly as the pink did. **The accent never passes for a severity** (13): the built-ins' hues keep out of the guarded bands, and the accent mark and every selection container stay ΔE ≥ 6 from every severity role, fill and container (measured 9.3 and 6.9 — 单色 has 苹果嘉儿's selection container at 2.5 from warning-container, which 多色 fixes). **Its pairs clear their bars everywhere** (11): the accent on the page 4.24:1, on the highest card 3.47, the level meter against its own track 3.45, the focus ring beside a selected row 4.97. **And nothing new is disliked** (12). The accent's light tone is **50, a mark tone**, not M3's 40, which turns every warm hue brown — `primary-ink` solves the same problem — so `on-tertiary` on `tertiary` is not a text pair; nothing uses it as one.

**A dark container is a shade, not a greyed tone** (ASSERTION 17, D1-010). A second colour is declared once, as the tint the light scheme's tone-90 containers wear (chroma 14–18), and M3 reuses that chroma at tone 30 in dark, where the warm and violet hues' gamut is two to four times as wide — so the 16 that is all of a tint's purity is a third of a shade's, and the rest is grey: 天琴 `#594234`, 苹果嘉儿 `#414a32`, 小蝶 `#5b3f4b`, 云宝 `#504158` and 默认/暮光's `#40465c` held 0.37–0.51 of their hue's deep register, where the four that read clean (邪茧, 露娜, 瑞瑞, 碧琪) held 0.68–0.81. So for the ten built-ins the dark 多色 secondary palette takes `deepSecondChroma` (`lib/paletteRule.ts`): the declared chroma or 65% of `clearChromaAt(hue, 30, 'deep')`, whichever is more, capped at C24 — two thirds of the accent's quiet end, so a broad selection stays quieter than a mark. That gives 天琴 `#613f2a`, 苹果嘉儿 `#3f4b2c`, 小蝶 `#613b4d`, 云宝 `#533f5f`, 默认/暮光 `#3c4665`; the clean four, the light scheme and 单色 (TonalSpot's, ASSERTION 8) do not move, and a saved custom theme keeps its derivation (the exact-custom contract), so the custom sweep is not held to it. It cannot make a dark orange not brown — tone 30 at H50 is a chestnut at any chroma; what it removes is the grey.

**Where 多色 deliberately does not reach**: the unread count (M3's error role, the fixed fill), the 近日推荐 badge (on a photograph, where only a non-flipping `primary` applies), the level badge Lv (beside role badges, it would read as a tag), links, switches and checkboxes (the brand's own marks).

**`error` deliberately does not follow M3, and that is not an oversight.** The spec pins it at hue 25 / chroma 84; this one is hue 22 / chroma 67.2 — the three degrees are worth nothing either way. What matters is that `success` (chroma 43.9) and `warning` (chroma 39.0) have **no M3 equivalent at all** (the spec has no such roles), and taking 84 for the one ramp that has a spec would make the error toast visibly louder than the success toast beside it — a severity ordering nobody chose. The three are tuned as a set.

**`rampChroma` is measured at `CHROMA_REFERENCE_TONE` (61), not at the fill's own tone, and that is the one non-obvious line in `lib/paletteRule.ts`.** A pale fill is pale because it sits where the gamut is *narrow* — `#FAF5AB` is chroma 30 at tone 95 where only 62 is available, while at tone 61 the same hue holds 67. Reading the chroma at the fill's tone built the entire primary palette at chroma 20 and made `primary-ink` — the colour of every glyph, tab indicator, checkbox and focused field outline in the app — come out `#a69166`, a khaki-grey; measured at tone 61 小蝶's ink is `#ac8e00`, a clean gold, and across the ten the inks run chroma 40–57 with no muddy member. ASSERTION 6 is that floor.

The consequence to carry: **a theme's fill and its ink are allowed to be very different colours** — 小蝶's bar is a butter yellow and her ink is a gold. That is correct (one answers a question about a surface and the other about a mark) and it is what lets a pale-coated character have a usable theme at all; every character's ink differs from its fill, because a decided fill sits off its own ramp.

### Measured limits and deliberate choices

The pale character fills have low boundary contrast against light pages; Luna/Rarity/Chrysalis dark bars have 1.77 / 2.13 / 2.39:1 against their dark page. Their text pairs still pass. These are reported fill-edge values, not a claim that every paint boundary reaches 3:1.

The two close-hue exemptions remain Luna/Rarity and Pinkie/default, held apart by measured fill distance. Character accent-container proximity to error-container remains about ΔE 4.9–5.3 for Lyra/Rainbow/Twilight in light, confined to the small filled favourite/like glyph; selection containers keep their stronger semantic-distance guard. The light tertiary remains the mark tone 50, not a normal-text pair. With the brand at H5.5 its primary family sits nearer the error hue: light `primary-container` (`#ffd9df`, the profile banner placeholder, a missing avatar, a drop zone while dragging) is ΔE 3.7 from `error-container`, and the dark link and `primary-ink` are ΔE 7.3 from `error`; the primary family is not held to ASSERTION 13.

Custom themes keep known contrast limits because the chosen colours and their existing derivation are not silently replaced. A focused outlined-field label therefore uses on-surface (invalid: error), not primary-ink; retain that shared field fix.

### Nineteen assertions

`npm run colors` checks tone/chroma fidelity, contrast floors/spread, readable source hue and hue separation, primary-ink chroma, disliked colours, exact TonalSpot mono ramps, built-in text pairs and direction-A chroma ceilings, multi-hue/semantic separation, accent/focus/chip pairs, the 99-seed custom sweep, tile/page separation, the dark selection container's clarity (17), the brand's literals (18) and the app bar's quieter ink (19). ### The eleventh palette: the user's own colour

One displayed hex is installed exactly. 选择颜色 names an exact colour; 从图片取色 recommends a colour after adjusting the image family's tone/chroma for the interface. Confirmation never transforms the displayed value again. Legacy single-seed cookies, manual values and saved themes retain their original derivation; no migration is performed.

**Under 多色 it has one 副色相, shared by the second colour and the accent** (decision 27 — the ten get two hues from an artist; one chosen by hand is the one least likely to clash). 自动 is a **right angle**: the seed's hue −90°, else +90°, never inside a guarded band (the three severities, each spanning its role and its fill, and the olive shoulder of `DislikeAnalyzer`'s band) — on the mascot's mane pink (`#e06c9f`, the brand's ramp seed) it lands on her own bows, 266° against 268°. Three presets are the same idea at other turns (邻近 −30°, 三分 −120°, 互补 180°, each with the same mirror and guard), and 自选 names a hue on a rail. **A relation tapers with the seed's confidence in its own hue** — a grey gets accents as grey as it is, as `rampChroma` gives it a near-grey ramp — while **a hue the user named is taken at its word**, guards included: the rule the primary already follows. The second colour is chroma 18 and the accent 36, the quiet end of the built-ins' ranges. 从图片取色 pairs each seed with the picture's own partner (below).

Nothing about it is generated, so it is **not** in `lib/generated/themeColors.ts`'s `PALETTES` array; that file carries only the id, because `PaletteId` is what every consumer validates against and a union that cannot express the palette the user chose is a union that silently downgrades them to the default.

Nine things about it are load-bearing:

- **The cookie carries the spec, not the declarations.** A stored *output* goes stale the moment the rule moves — exactly what happened to the ten twice — and re-deriving costs one HCT run, memoised by `deriveCustomThemeCached`.
- **The server derives it.** `lib/paletteRule.ts` is a plain module, so `app/layout.tsx` ships every declaration, 多色 and 单色, in the first byte. No client equivalent: the pre-paint script runs before any stylesheet resolves and cannot carry HCT, and the lazy chunk lands after several paints.
- **A `<style>`, never inline properties on `<html>`.** Inline beats every selector including `html.dark[data-palette='custom']`, so a scheme flip would keep painting the light values. As a stylesheet the specificity is (0,1,1)/(0,2,1) — exactly as the generated file — so `applyScheme` needs no knowledge of it, and the rules come from the same `paletteBlocksCss` as `theme-palettes.css`: they cannot diverge.
- **The pre-paint script's `C` object is the validation list**, gaining `custom` only when *this request's* cookie carried a usable spec; a stored `custom` with nothing rendered for it falls back to `default` rather than a palette no stylesheet answers to (one line, malformed and cleared-cookie alike), and the shell then re-derives it from storage (`recoverCustomPalette`, which also catches a cookie naming another spec than storage).
- **The tile cannot read the tokens** — they are always the *active* theme's, so the first chip turned 露娜's blue the moment you selected 露娜. `applyCustomPalette` parks fourteen hexes on `<html>` as `data-palette-tones` — `primary`/`on-primary` per scheme, then per scheme the face's page, selection and accent and 单色's page and selection, packed by `packTones` (`lib/paletteSpec.ts`), which `app/layout.tsx` uses at SSR too — with the seed and 副色相 beside them, left there when you switch away.
- **A `dynamic()` dialog needs `loading`, and its absence is a page-level bug.** Both dialogs are `dynamic()` — together a 32-cell gamut-aware grid, a hue rail and deterministic area-ranked image recommendation analysis — and without `loading` the component **suspends** to the *route's* boundary: the first press of 选择颜色 replaced all of /settings with its route skeleton. `loading: () => null` moves the boundary onto the dialog, whose own skeleton covers the gap. Each also holds a one-way "has ever opened" latch, or `dynamic()` fetches at mount and a user who only names a colour still pays for the image-analysis chunk; each chunk is instead fetched on *intent* — a pointer arriving on, pressing, or focus reaching the button that opens it. A confirmed colour is installed from `Modal`'s `onExited`, not at the confirm: the wipe snapshots the screen, and a dialog still leaving was frozen into the old theme's picture and dragged away with it. The wipe grows from the button that opened that dialog.
- **The same picture gives the same colours.** The library's TypeScript `QuantizerCelebi` starts its k-means by putting every pixel in a `Math.random` cluster, and its first pass prunes from that start, so one picture offered different colours on every read (the mascot's second render: 8 distinct answers in 20). `lib/quantize.ts` is the port with that one line replaced — the starts drawn from `java.util.Random` seeded `0x42688`, as AOSP's Java quantizer draws them — and `scripts/testPalette.mjs` holds both the generator's reference values and the repeat-read invariant. Never go back to the library's quantizer for this.
- **Area ranks recommendations.** `lib/imageRecommendations.ts` groups C≥8 deterministic clusters around fixed 22° hue anchors, sums covered opaque pixels, then ranks families by area. Chroma does not give a small vivid detail priority. Tone/chroma use approved A: pale T82–88 (warm red/peach up to 86; yellow up to 92); a cool H165–325 family with ≥60% below T50 may recommend T34–42 first. Each family gets a place before supported light/deep alternatives, up to eight.
- **Every opaque sample is counted.** Final Lab centres that round to the same RGB key have their populations added. Assignments and centres are preserved; there is no nearest-centre recount. The 92-pixel negative control formerly returned only 71.
- **No invented fallback hue.** A gray image keeps its own populous tones, spread 12 tones apart. An all-transparent image gives no recommendations; an unreadable file is a separate decoding error. The old Monet helpers remain pure compatibility/reference utilities and do not drive the product picker.

- **The downsample is an area, not an edge**: `WallpaperColors.java` caps `MAX_WALLPAPER_EXTRACTION_AREA = 112 × 112` and rescales by `sqrt(cap / area)` ("aspect ratio independent"); it was a 128px longest edge. One deliberate divergence: `imageSmoothingQuality: 'high'` where AOSP scales with `filter = false` (nearest neighbour) — at a 30× reduction it samples one pixel in nine hundred and can delete the small saturated subject that then trips the cutoff above.

**Recommendation and exact selection are separate operations.** Direction A acts only while extracting recommendations. It retains the input family's hue and chooses a clear light/deep register before showing the theme; no style setting is stored. Manual colours and already saved specs do not pass through it.

**An image companion comes from an eligible family, otherwise it is honestly automatic.** Candidates need ≥0.2% of opaque pixels, readable chroma, a ≥45° hue gap and the existing automatic hue guards. Area leads; among populations within 10% of the largest eligible one, separation nearest 90° breaks the tie. One editable companion hue serves secondary and tertiary; mono keeps it for a later switch while showing its one-hue theme. The dialog explains recommendation adjustments and automatic fallback.

- **Two dialogs rather than one.** One dialog with two entry points held two models at once (a `picked` option *and* a hex, each clearing the other) and a confirm button ambiguous about which it was sending. **`primary` is a fill role; `primary-ink` is the brand as a mark.** Same hue, at whichever tone can be read on the page. **Links follow the palette, and the underline is what pays for it.** The old fixed blue left every link ignoring the theme (blue is a link's affordance, but the argument left the source URL under a picture off-theme too). They are the brand hue at a text tone now (6.12–6.19:1 on light `surface`, 10.81–10.94:1 dark, vs the old blue's 6.34 / 10.09 — a hue swap with no legibility change), and prose links carry a **rest-state underline** so the affordance no longer rests on hue. The underline is what pays for the swap: under the old blue a link sat 22.6° from 云宝黛西's brand and 23.0° from 瑞瑞's in OKLab hue — close enough to read as one colour — and now it *is* that hue (0.2°–2.1° from `primary-ink` across the ten), so the line is the only thing left distinguishing a link from an emphasis mark.

The selectors carry `html` (`html[data-palette='x']`, `html.dark[data-palette='x']`) and that is load-bearing: `@import` has to precede every rule, so the generated blocks land *above* `:root`, and `:root` and `[data-palette=x]` are both specificity (0,1,0). With the element name they are (0,1,1)/(0,2,1) and beat `:root` and `.dark` whatever the order. 单色 adds one attribute to each — (0,2,1)/(0,3,1) — and carries only the roles it changes (eight per character; the default's twenty-two, its coat included), declared as one set in both halves of the pair. **globals.css holds the default theme's 多色**, so a visitor with no cookie needs no selector at all; 多色 is the attribute's *absence*. The two blocks per scheme cost about 0.7 KB brotli over the ten.

To **add** a theme: one entry in `THEMES` in `scripts/palette.mjs` — an id, a label, the fill's decision (guide hue, tone, share of the gamut) with the guide row it came from, and 多色's second colour and accent with theirs — then `npm run colors:write`; the CSS, the tiles' colours (`lib/generated/themeColors.ts` for the bar, `themeFaces.ts` for the rest) and the `<meta name="theme-color">` values are all products of that run, and ASSERTIONS 9–13 say whether the choices hold. To **move the brand**: edit the default row's `fill` decision in `THEMES` and run it — ASSERTION 18 prints the two literals to paste into `BRAND_SEED`/`BRAND_SEED_DARK` in `lib/paletteSpec.ts` (the import-free module, so a client that needs only the colour reads the one literal; `lib/paletteRule.ts` re-exports both) — then re-check `.logo-keyline` against the Lottie artwork. `BRAND_RAMP_SEED` beside them is the mascot's mane, `#e06c9f`, and does not move with the fill: `BRAND_CHROMA`, the floor under every theme's ramp, is read off it, and a tint's chroma would take all eleven `primary-ink`s down (ASSERTION 18 holds it to chroma ≥ 35). A new role goes into globals.css by hand once, in both blocks (the run names it and its value); after that `--write` keeps it. The hand-copy step (two literals in `viewport.themeColor`) is gone: that export carries no `themeColor` at all, because a static array cannot express eleven palettes (one per-user) and mutating Next's own tag does not survive a client navigation — the tag is rendered from the cookie in `app/layout.tsx`.

The progress row had drifted furthest. `LinearProgressIndicatorTokens` puts `Height`, `TrackThickness` and `ActiveThickness` all at **4dp** — no size axis; the track is `secondary-container`; the indicator is `secondary` or a `*-fill`; the fill is a full-width rounded bar translated out of the clipping track (`translateX` of the value less 100%), not an animated `width`, because `width` is a layout property and one instance runs live inside the image overlay while a flight is landing. The dialog was `surface-container-lowest`, the *flattest* step: the one surface meant to read as lifted off everything was painted lighter than the page behind it and relied entirely on its shadow. Tone first, shadow second is the whole M3 depth recipe; that had it backwards.

The tooltip is the one place `inverse-surface` is right — no tension with the snackbar's note below. `inverse-surface` flips between schemes, which is wrong for a **severity**: the same message must not arrive as a dark chip in one theme and a light one in the other. A tooltip carries no severity; its whole job is to contrast with whatever surface it is over, and flipping is how it keeps doing that.

**An alpha on a token is a bug.** `bg-primary/10`, `border-error/40`, `text-on-media/60` — each eyeballed once per scheme, each drifting, because nothing makes the next call site pick the same number; the tint that looked right on light is nearly invisible over dark. Use the tone scale (`surface-container-*`) or the container/on-container pair. If you need a weight no token has, add the token.

**There is now exactly one legitimate alpha at a call site**, and it is not a colour: the `--md-sys-state-*` opacities, M3's own `StateTokens` numbers, reaching call sites through `state-layer` rather than by hand. Two former entries were listed "legitimate" and were wrong:

- `bg-scrim/50`, "one value, three call sites": the value is **0.32** (`ScrimTokens.ContainerOpacity`), so all three were 56% too heavy — and a fourth, at 62%, hid inside an arbitrary `shadow-[…]` in `ImageCropper`. The dim is `bg-scrim-veil` now and the crop mask is `--md-sys-color-crop-mask`: two different objects, two tokens, no number at a call site. A crop mask being heavier than a dialog dim is a real distinction — it has to make "outside the crop" inactive while staying legible enough to aim with.
- `bg-on-surface-variant/40` on the sheet's drag handle was not an M3 number at all: `SheetBottomTokens.DockedDragHandleColor` is `OnSurfaceVariant`, full strength, and `SheetDefaults` passes it unmodified.

The fix has the same shape every time: need a weight no token has → **add the token**; and if the thing you are weighting turns out to be a different object from the one the existing token serves, that is why the number kept drifting.

**An element `opacity-NN` used as an ink weight is the same bug.** The rule above only named alphas *on tokens*, so the identical drift grew back: a quoted reply preview at 70% in two components that each picked the number alone, an English tag name at 50%, a footer wordmark at 60%, a banner icon at 80% — none could agree, because there was nothing to agree with. Reach for a *type role* first (a supporting line is `body-s` under a `body-m`, not a faded copy of it), then `on-surface-variant`, then add a token. Element opacity is legitimate for motion (a fade, a hover reveal, a collapsing `grid-rows-[0fr]`), for the paging dim over a grid whose next page is on its way (one value, 50%, at every paged grid), and for `disabled-content`.

**Disabled content is `disabled-content` (38%), and nothing else.** M3 specifies 38%; the app used five weights — 40 on `IconButton` and `Pagination`'s arrows, 50 on `Button`, `Chip`, `Checkbox`, `ToggleSwitch`, `Select`'s trigger and `DropZone`, 60 on `Input`, 70 on the captcha handle, a lone 38 inside `Select`'s menu — with two files disagreeing with *themselves* (`Pagination`'s arrows vs its numbers; `Select`'s trigger vs the menu it opens). It is a `@utility` in globals.css rather than a colour so a control's border, glyph, label and container fade together in one declaration, and it composes with the variant: `disabled:disabled-content`.

| Primary ink — a title, a tag chip   | `text-on-media`      |
| Secondary ink — caption, meta, +N   | `text-on-media-variant` |

`bg-media-plate` is the one answer for a format badge, a score pill, a caption bar or a hover veil. It replaced `bg-scrim/40`, `/50`, `/55` and `/60` — four weights for one object, the two closest on screen the furthest apart.

**Focus** is one ring for the whole app: `focus-visible:ring-2 focus-ring`, or `focus-ring-on-primary` for chrome on the app bar. Over a **photograph** it is `focus-visible:inset-ring-2 focus-ring-on-media`, and the inward direction is not optional: that ring resolves to `on-media` (white), and an outset white ring puts its outer edge on the picture where a bright subject takes it to 1:1; inward it sits on the control's own `bg-media-plate`, where the worst case — a pure-white subject under a 55% black plate — still measures 4.8:1. `IconButton variant="media"` supplies that plate and switches the ring width itself; anything else reaching for this needs one too. Never tint it per variant — a focus ring answers "where is the keyboard", so it must look identical on every control — and it is solid, not tinted: `primary/40` composites to about 1.4:1 against a light surface, under the 3:1 WCAG 2.4.11 asks of a focus indicator.

**The ring is `secondary`** — M3's own name: `FocusIndicatorColor` is `Secondary` in `MenuTokens`, `SearchBarTokens`, the card tokens and the chip tokens alike. It was `primary`: on a filled button that was `primary` on `primary`, **1:1** — the app's most prominent control had no visible focus state — and against the light surfaces 2.94:1 / 2.39:1, both under the 3:1 bar; `secondary` measures 6.17:1 / 5.01:1 light and 10.90:1 / 7.26:1 dark. The two exceptions exist because neither role has any guaranteed contrast on the brand bar or over a picture. **It is TonalSpot's secondary in both 配色方案** — a literal per theme rather than the `secondary` token, which under 多色 is the theme's second colour: the ring answers where the keyboard is, and choosing a scheme must not change that answer (ASSERTION 14).

**An outset ring is drawn on the surface *behind* the control, not on the control** — do not "fix" `Button variant="filled"` to use `focus-ring-on-primary`. `ring-2` is a `box-shadow` painted just outside the border box: on a filled button those pixels are the page, where `secondary` measures 6.15:1. The 2.09:1 figure in globals.css's table is for a ring drawn *on* a primary-coloured surface — the app bar, and nowhere else — which is what `focus-ring-on-primary` is for; `IconButton variant="on-primary"` already carries it.

The third form is `focus-visible:inset-ring-2 focus-visible:focus-ring-inset` — the same ring painted inward, not a second style. A ring is a `box-shadow`, so an ancestor that paint-contains throws it away entirely, and the app has two kinds that do: a gallery card (`.image-card` is `contain: layout paint style`) and any media box clipping its corners with `overflow-hidden rounded-*`. A full-bleed control inside one of those — the spoiler cover, a zoom target — rendered a focus indicator that was then discarded. Reach for it only when the enclosure clips; everywhere else the ring goes outside, where it does not eat 2px of the control.

The fourth form is for a control that floats over whatever the page shows and has no plate of its own — the mascot and the ponies: `focus-visible:ring-2 focus-visible:ring-offset-2 focus-visible:ring-offset-surface focus-ring`, the same ring with a 2px band of the page's `surface` inside it. Over a picture the band gives the ring the edge it has everywhere else (6.17:1 light, 10.90:1 dark). A media plate would not do: a pony's sprite is drawn in the frame beneath its control, so a plate on the control darkens the pony (G1-022).

**Forced colors (Windows High Contrast) drop every ring**, because a ring is a `box-shadow`, and flatten every container tone. One rule beside the focus rings in globals.css draws a 2px `Highlight` outline on `:focus-visible` instead — inset where the control's ring is inset, on the field's own boundary for a field, and on the keyboard's roving cursor, which has no focus of its own. The primitives hide the engine's outline with `outline-hidden`, which Tailwind keeps as a transparent outline under forced colors (so the control is right even without that rule), never `outline-none`, which removes it there too. Three utilities say what a part becomes in that mode: `forced-boundary` (a 1px system-text edge for anything that separated itself by tone), `forced-selected` (a selected container takes the system's selection pair) and `forced-mark` (a mark that exists only as paint — a tab indicator, a switch track, a radio dot, a meter's fill — takes `Highlight`, or it vanishes and takes the state with it). Their colours are `!important` inside the media query because a utility's place in the stylesheet follows its first property, which sorted them ahead of the container utilities they have to beat: measured, the selected rail tab kept its pink.

The **outlined text field** has no outer ring: focus thickens its own outline to 2px in `primary-ink`, while an invalid outlined field retains the `error` outline and label. A control whose visual boundary *is* a 1px outline cannot wear a ring around it without reading as two nested boxes. The *filled* field has no outline to nest inside, so it takes the ordinary `focus` ring, which takes precedence over its error ring. This is the field family's own boundary treatment; it does not change the focus roles of other controls.

**`outline` is a boundary role, not an ink role.** It is built for a rule or a text-field border and is specified to 3:1 — the bar for a *non-text* element. Against this app's light surface it lands at 4.3:1, under the 4.5:1 WCAG AA asks of normal-size text; the dark scheme reaches 5.8:1 and passes. That asymmetry is why `text-outline` spread to 57 pieces of supporting text unnoticed: it is only wrong in one scheme, the one people ship from less often. Supporting text is `on-surface-variant` (8.5–9:1 light, 10:1 dark). `text-outline` on a *glyph* is fine — 4.3:1 clears the 3:1 non-text bar.

**A state is a container, not a rule down the side.** The reflex for an unread notification, a selected row, a quoted reply is a 3–4px coloured bar at the leading edge; M3 has no such element — a list item says "unread"/"selected" by wearing a container pair, and a block of text says "quoted" by `<blockquote>`'s own treatment. So an unread system message is `secondary-container` / `on-secondary-container` across the whole row, and the bar is gone. The ink half is not optional: the row's title and body must *inherit* the on-container colour, so write `text-on-surface`/`text-on-surface-variant` into the read branch only — a hard `text-on-surface` on the heading survives the container change and leaves a coloured row whose text still belongs to the old one.

The reply quote keeps its rule, because there the bar is not a state — it is what distinguishes quoted text from the reply around it, and what `<blockquote>` has looked like for thirty years. It is 4px `primary` (the same colour as the `<cite>` under it, so quote and attribution read as one object) over a `surface-container-high` fill — the same tone step as everything else here meaning "a distinct block inside this one".

**A filter control that is not selected is a tone step, not a keyline.** `Select`, the filled text field and `Button variant="surface"` share `surface-container-highest` without a border or decorative shadow. An unselected `Chip` remains `surface-container-high`, a different control role. Use the field toolbar family when those neighbours need one material, rather than adding a chip just because the control expands something.
